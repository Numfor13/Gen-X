# Cameroon: the informal artisan / blue-collar trades problem and the customer side of hiring (problem-context brief, as of 12 Nov 2026)

> Scope: Cameroon only (other African examples are labelled "COMPARISON"). Every claim has an inline source; older data is labelled with its year. Research note: the shared web-search budget for this research run ran out partway through this researcher's work. Later findings came from fetching primary pages and PDFs directly (INS, DataReportal, the ART regulator, press, Frontiers). Items that could not be verified are listed under "Gaps".

## 1. How big is Cameroon's informal economy and its artisan segment, and how is it structured (size, gender, age, urbanisation)?

### Takeaway
Informal work is the norm in Cameroon. The INS EESI3 survey (2021) puts 86.6% of all jobs in the informal sector, and 52.0% in informal *non-agricultural* activity (67.5% in urban areas). The country has about 3.4–3.5 million informal production units (UPIs). They are tiny (about 1.2 people each), 56% are headed by women, and 64% are urban. Only 6% of informal workers are wage earners, and only 2% have a written contract. Nobody has a reliable count of "artisans" specifically: official artisan registers capture only a few thousand to about 72,000 units, against millions of informal workers.

### Cited Findings
**Informal share of employment (INS EESI3, fieldwork May–July 2021)**
- 86.6% of jobs are in the informal sector: 52.0% informal non-agricultural, 34.7% informal agricultural, 8.2% public sector and 5.1% formal private sector. — [INS EESI3 Phase 1 main report page](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/)
- In urban areas, 67.5% of employed people work in the informal non-agricultural sector. Informal non-agricultural work peaks at 62.8% among workers aged 20–24 (59.4% among 14–19-year-olds). — [INS EESI3 Phase 1 full report (PDF, FR)](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/EESI3_Rapport-principal_Emploi_VF.pdf)
- Labour force participation (14+) is 54.2% and the employment rate 50.8% (men 58.3%, women 43.7%). Participation is 59.2% in Douala and 55.6% in Yaoundé. Salaried employment is 48.5% in Douala and 64.6% in Yaoundé. About 3 in 10 employed people in each city are migrants. Only 11 in 100 workers have insurance cover through their main job, and 9 in 100 reported a work accident in their main job. — [INS EESI3 Phase 1 page](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/)
- Unemployment (ILO definition, 14+) is 6.1% nationally but 15.4% in Douala and 11.7% in Yaoundé (9.4% across urban areas). It rises with education: 17.6% among people with higher education. Overall underemployment fell from 70.6% (2010) to 61.4% (2021). Wage jobs as a share of informal non-agricultural employment fell from 41.3% (2010) to 32.6% (2021). — [INS EESI3 Phase 1 full report](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/EESI3_Rapport-principal_Emploi_VF.pdf)
- A secondary source citing the World Bank (2023) gives about 90% informal: 45% in informal agriculture and 39% outside agriculture. It also says agriculture fell from about 66–70% of employment (2000) to about 43% (2023–24), while services rose to about 43%. — [Nkafu Policy Institute](https://nkafu.org/beyond-the-trade-deficit-and-the-jobs-crisis-breaking-the-vicious-circle-of-precariousness-through-industrialization/); [CEPI State of Entrepreneurship 2024/25](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/)

**Informal production units (UPIs), EESI3 Phase 2 (2021)**
- There are more than 3.4 million UPIs: 63.7% urban and 36.3% rural. By sector, 36.4% are services, 35.2% commerce and 28.4% industry (which includes crafts and construction). Women head 56.1% of units, and the average unit is 8.5 years old. — [Cameroon Tribune, 8 Nov 2023, citing INS](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- A May 2026 op-ed cites INS for 3.5 million UPIs. — [Camer.be op-ed, L. M. Kakdeu, 30 May 2026](https://www.camer.be/93491/30:27/cameroun-creer-une-chambre-de-metiers-comme-levier-de-transition-vers-une-economie-formelle-cameroon.html)
- Only 6% of informal workers are wage earners (9% in 2010). The wage share is 7.7% in Douala and 4.9% in Yaoundé. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- Units run by one person alone make up 78.3% of the total. 52.3% of workers have some form of contract, but only 2.1% are written (50.2% are verbal). — [Ecofin Agency](http://www.ecofinagency.com/public-management/0611-45017-cameroons-informal-workers-earn-an-average-of-cfa83-409-per-month-ins)
- Average monthly income is CFA 83,409 (urban 96,367; rural 59,520), or CFA 669 per hour. Employers earn about CFA 181,200 a month and own-account workers about CFA 93,500. Average capital per unit is CFA 378,400, but the median is only CFA 36,000. Between 2010 and 2021, average investment rose most in transport (+CFA 579,400) and repairs (+CFA 251,400). — [Ecofin Agency](http://www.ecofinagency.com/public-management/0611-45017-cameroons-informal-workers-earn-an-average-of-cfa83-409-per-month-ins); [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- Start-up funding: own savings 55.9%, gifts or inheritance 16.3%, **tontines/njangis 12.9%**, borrowing from relatives 8.5%. Formal finance is rare. Only 15.1% of unit heads had heard of social security. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- The INS leaflet (2021 columns) shows roughly 1.2–1.3 people per unit on average, a median monthly income of about CFA 40,000, about 69.5% of units with no capital, an average of about 6 years of schooling, and about 71% of unit heads saying they are "ready to register". (These figures were read from a layout-garbled PDF extraction and should be checked against the original.) — [INS EESI3 Phase 2 leaflet (PDF, FR)](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/EESI3_Depliant_Secteur-informel_FR_28_09_2023-2.pdf); [leaflet landing page](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-2-informal-sector-survey-main-indicators-leaflet/)
- INS defines the informal sector as production units with no taxpayer number and/or no formal (OHADA) accounts. The survey covered 4,762 non-agricultural units, of which 4,576 were interviewed. — [INS leaflet page](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-2-informal-sector-survey-main-indicators-leaflet/)

**Informal share of GDP (estimates conflict)**
- About 60% of GDP and "nearly 90% of the active population" (op-ed, no primary citation) — [Camer.be, Kakdeu 2026](https://www.camer.be/93491/30:27/cameroun-creer-une-chambre-de-metiers-comme-levier-de-transition-vers-une-economie-formelle-cameroon.html). This conflicts with about 40% of GDP (no source given) in [CEPI 2024/25](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/) and with "over 70% of activity" in [Nkafu](https://nkafu.org/beyond-the-trade-deficit-and-the-jobs-crisis-breaking-the-vicious-circle-of-precariousness-through-industrialization/).
- The IMF (2024) says a "large portion of the business community remains in the informal sector" but gives no GDP share. — [IMF Country Report 2024](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1cmrea2024002.pdf.md)

**Artisans specifically**
- The Minister of SMEs, Social Economy and Handicrafts (MINPMEESA) says crafts account for "more than 50%" of Cameroon's workers. The crafts share of GDP is put at about 5% by "concordant data" the ministry does not confirm. Artisan production units grew from 11,814 (2019) to 72,508 (2025) and make up 12.8% of the ministry's SME stock. COMPARISON: Côte d'Ivoire's crafts sector is 19.5% of GDP, has about 4 million workers and 245 trades in 8 branches. — [EcoMatin, 11 Aug 2026](https://ecomatin.net/artisanat-le-cameroun-veut-sinspirer-du-modele-ivoirien-pour-booster-sa-contribution-au-pib)
- CONFLICT: the MINPMEESA 2022 statistical yearbook counted only **5,912 artisan production units (UPA) registered** with communal crafts offices in 2022 (down 3.59%). 26.49% were in the Far North, and women made up 51.38% of registrations there. The total enterprise stock was 350,422, 97% of them sole proprietorships. — [Journal du Cameroun, 1 Jun 2023](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/entrepreneuriat-pres-de-350-000-pme-estimees-au-cameroun-en-2022/). Annual registrations (5,912) and the cumulative stock (72,508) are probably different measures; neither is close to the true artisan population.
- Over 444,000 registered enterprises (2024). — [CEPI](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/)
- Douala's informal economy is described as large in cloth-making, construction and car repair. Ndokoti, Bepanda, Village and Logbaba are named as areas where informal workers are most visibly deprived. — [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala)

**Demography and urbanisation**
- Population is 30.8 million (Oct 2026), growing 2.5% a year, with a median age of 18.2. DataReportal shows 76.2% urban (late 2026). — [DataReportal Digital 2027: Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-cameroon). CONFLICT: the previous edition gave 60.6% urban (late 2025) — [DataReportal Digital 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon) — and the CIA gave 59.3% (2023), as summarised in search results. The jump probably reflects the new UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 definition, not real change.
- UN WUP 2025 agglomeration estimates for 2026: Yaoundé 5.24M (+2.64%/yr), Douala 4.20M (+2.33%), Garoua 691k, Maroua 536k, Bamenda 397k (+0.70%, a slowdown linked to the conflict), Kumba 342k (+3.60%), Bafoussam 324k, Limbe 242k (+3.45%), Buea 232k (+3.89%). — [Worldometer / UN WUP 2025](https://www.worldometers.info/population/largest-cities-in-cameroon/)
- City-proper figures (INS-based) put Douala at about 3.82M and Yaoundé at about 3.76M (2025), so Douala is slightly larger on that measure. — [citypopulation.de](https://citypopulation.de/en/cameroon/cities/?admid=6817); [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala)
- Poverty: about 4 in 10 people live below the national poverty line, and 23.0% below the extreme international line (2021–22). GDP growth was about 3.4% in 2025 and is projected at 3.7% for 2026–28. — [World Bank Cameroon overview](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/cameroon/overview). National income poverty in 2022 was 37.7% (urban 21.6%, rural 56.3%). — [INS ECAM5 SDG sheet (PDF)](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Page-ODD-ECAM5-En.pdf)

### Inferences
- The artisan "market" is huge but atomised. Most providers are one-person, own-account operators with no premises, no written contracts, no capital and no social protection. They cannot signal quality through formal means (licences, insurance, company brands), which leaves a structural trust vacuum.
- High urban unemployment (Douala 15.4%) alongside 61% underemployment means plenty of supply: many artisans have idle capacity and want more jobs. The constraint is matching and trust, not the number of workers.
- Women head 56% of UPIs, but the hands-on building trades (electrical, plumbing, masonry, mechanics) are male-dominated, while tailoring and hairdressing are female-dominated (see §5). Which trade a pilot picks largely decides the gender profile of its suppliers.

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of artisans by trade (electricians, plumbers, masons, tailors, mechanics) exists in public sources. The EESI3 branch tables (Phase 2 full report) could not be retrieved: the URL returned an HTML error page.
- No ILOSTAT informality rate was retrieved: the World Bank and ILO APIs were unreachable during this session.
- Informal GDP share: only conflicting secondary estimates (40%, 60%, >70%) were found.
- No source was found for the share of UPIs working from roadside or itinerant posts versus fixed workshops by trade (only the leaflet's garbled "% with premises" columns).

## 2. How do artisans win work today, and how do customers find and vet them?

### Takeaway
Work flows through personal networks: referrals from neighbours, colleagues and family, the trainer or "patron" an artisan learned under, phone numbers painted on poles and walls, and walk-up visits to roadside workshops. Classifieds (CoinAfrique, Jiji) and Facebook and WhatsApp sharing add a thin digital layer, but they carry no verification. Customers vet by asking who referred the artisan and who trained them, and by seeing past work. Several Cameroonian startups (Maibeta since 2015, OmniJobber, Quicky, QwickServices, Nofey, TrustWork, FixIt) have tried to digitise this. None has visibly reached scale.

### Cited Findings
- National data on how jobseekers search for work (2021): personal relations 34.5% (down from 42.9% in 2010), small ads in newspapers, radio and posters 23.9% (up from 7.2%), approaching employers directly 22.8%, competitive exams 6.8%, employment agencies (FNE, BMO) only 1.1%. — [INS EESI3 Phase 1 full report](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/EESI3_Rapport-principal_Emploi_VF.pdf)
- Maibeta (Buea, founded 2015): households and businesses rely on informal artisans but face "inconsistent pricing, limited accountability, and uncertainty about work quality". Customers "often rely on a recommendation from a neighbour, colleague, or friend", and artisans depend on word of mouth. The core problem is "a lack of a trusted system", not a lack of skilled workers. — [Tony Elumelu Foundation profile of Maibeta](https://www.tonyelumelufoundation.org/?p=29130)
- Maibeta (2018 data): before the platform, customers found technicians through "contacts on utility poles" or friends and family. It had 1,000+ verified technicians, 2,300+ jobs, about 75% of transactions through the web portal and **about 25% through WhatsApp, SMS and phone calls**. Its problems were copycat competitors and high customer acquisition costs. — [MIT Solve: Maibeta](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/3333)
- OmniJobber (Yaoundé, 2018): "about 90%" of the labour force is informal, orders are infrequent and processes "unsecured". Buyers need vetted providers quickly. It recruited providers by **field "hawking" and door-to-door registration**, offered SMS, voice and USSD access, and charged an 8–20% commission. It had about 200 vetted workers and 500+ requests, mostly in Yaoundé, Douala and Buea. — [MIT Solve: OmniJobber](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/2777)
- Buea tailoring study (2022): workshops cluster in **Molyko near the University of Buea**, whose staff and students are the customers. Trainers say "the quality of my work advertises itself" (no radio adverts). Apprentices say: "When people know your trainer, they become confident in you… that's why you will always see certificates and pictures in most shops." So the trainer's lineage and graduation certificates work as the local trust signal. — [Ngyah-Etchutambe et al., Frontiers in Education 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1027632/pdf)
- QwickServices (GAN Tech Consulting) launched in Douala on 20 Apr 2026. It operates in Douala, Yaoundé, Buea, Bamenda and Limbe, claims 2,000+ categories (plumbing, electrical work and more), escrow-style payments released when the customer confirms the job, ratings and "verified reviews", nearly 1,000 verified providers and "hundreds" of pilot transactions. It frames the market as affected by **fraud** and lacking structure. The Fon of Mankon sits on its board, a traditional-authority endorsement. — [Cameroon Tribune, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76197/en.html/details_2)
- Quicky Services (Yaoundé, 2025): background-checked providers, prices shown upfront, mobile money **and cash** accepted, about 500+ downloads. Its stated challenges are reliability, quality control and coverage of less dense areas. — [WeAreTech Africa, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/cameroons-quicky-services-bridges-clients-and-providers-via-mobile-app)
- Nofey (co-founded by Cameroonian Audrey Ludivine Baha, 2025): an app with ratings, reviews and mobile money for plumbing, electrical work, mechanics, masonry and other trades. It names no operating cities and gives no user numbers. — [WeAreTech Africa, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/audrey-ludivine-baha-launches-nofey-to-digitize-access-to-local-artisans-in-africa)
- FixIt (TotalEnergies Startupper entry, 2024) uses the example of "Awa", a plumber in Yaoundé who depends on word of mouth, has unpredictable income and "no way to build a reputation". — [TotalEnergies Startupper: FixIt](https://startupper.totalenergies.com/en/juries/86Y55jNNiLsd7YcnUUiwwQ/participations/9485/vote)
- TrustWork is an iOS app for finding plumbers, carpenters and electricians in Cameroon, with too few ratings to display (per search listing). — [App Store: TrustWork](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/trustwork/id6745813388)
- CoinAfrique Cameroon has a "Services" category ("travaux/bricolage/jardinage", cleaning, moving and others) but no artisan category, and no verification beyond safety tips such as "do not send payments without having verified" and meeting in public. Listings are concentrated in Douala and Yaoundé. — [CoinAfrique Cameroun](https://cm.coinafrique.com/)
- A low-quality foreign listing site shows indicative Douala prices of US$50–200 for plumbing and US$75–300 for electrical jobs (unverified). — [ProGigFinder Douala plumbing](https://www.progigfinder.com/services/plumbing/douala); [electrical](https://www.progigfinder.com/services/electrical/douala)
- Diaspora owners who build remotely rely on a relative as their "eyes in the country". WhatsApp videos and Facebook posts gave a falsely positive picture of a building site before the relative took control of the property (case study, 2017). — [Camer.be, 28 Jun 2017](https://www.camer.be/61065/15:1/cameroon-cameroun-lorsque-les-biens-dun-mbenguiste-sement-la-zizanie-dans-la-famille.html)

### Inferences
- Trust markers already exist in local practice: the referrer's identity, the trainer/patron lineage, graduation certificates on workshop walls, and the physical workshop location ("everyone in the quartier knows him"). An authentic reputation engine should digitise these signals (vouching chains, master-apprentice lineage, neighbourhood attestations) instead of importing star ratings alone.
- Operators who have tried this report the same problems: high acquisition cost, quality control, copycats, and a large share of activity running through WhatsApp, SMS and calls. A WhatsApp/USSD-first design is a market requirement, not an optional extra.
- At least 7 Cameroonian attempts and no breakout winner suggests the bottleneck is liquidity and verification depth in a dense area, not app availability. That supports a hyper-local pilot.

### Gaps
- No source quantified artisan use of Facebook groups, WhatsApp groups or Jiji. jiji.cm did not resolve from this environment, and Facebook groups cannot be fetched.
- No survey was found on *how customers vet* artisans (for example, the share relying on referrals versus classifieds). The evidence is qualitative (startup pitches, one academic study).
- No data was found on the traction or survival of Maibeta and OmniJobber after 2018.
- No primary source was found on quarter chiefs (*chefs de quartier/bloc*) or market leaders formally vouching for artisans. This is plausible from local governance practice but unverified here.

## 3. What goes wrong? Documented pain points (bad hires, abandoned jobs, poor workmanship, fires and collapses, price opacity, advance-payment fraud)

### Takeaway
The documented failures fall into three groups. Safety failures: house and market fires blamed on chaotic wiring, low-end electrical equipment and power-cut surges, and repeated building collapses blamed on substandard construction (33 dead in 2023; 6 dead in Bonamoussadi, Douala, in July 2026). Reliability failures: missed deadlines, abandoned sites, artisans "disappearing" with advances. Price failures: inconsistent, opaque pricing. Hard statistics are scarce. Most evidence is press reporting and incident coverage, and there is no national fire or electrocution registry.

### Cited Findings
**Electrical fires and electrocution**
- A June 2026 article on household electrocution says "no exhaustive national register" of domestic electrocution accidents is publicly available. It recommends "calling a certified electrician" and a professional audit of old installations. — [Camer.be, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.camer.be/amp/93595/11:1/cameroun-rallonge-defectueuse-le-danger-silencieux-qui-tue-dans-nos-maisons-cameroon.html)
- A search-result summary attributes to the Environment Ministry the claim that "85% of fires recorded in Cameroon are of electrical origin", and says domestic installations are "often carried out by non-professionals, with low-end equipment and without earthing". On direct fetch of the article this summary pointed to, neither claim appeared, so treat the figure as **unverified**. — [Camer.be (fetched article did not contain the figure)](https://www.camer.be/amp/93595/11:1/cameroun-rallonge-defectueuse-le-danger-silencieux-qui-tue-dans-nos-maisons-cameroon.html)
- Fires are rising in Yaoundé, Douala and Bafoussam (Aug 2025). Douala's Mboppi market burned "at least three times" in 2025. Causes cited include "low-end electrical equipment", power cuts and "bad electrical connections". — [Le360 Afrique, 31 Aug 2025](https://afrique.le360.ma/societe/cameroun-linquietante-recrudescence-des-incendies-dans-les-grandes-villes-du-pays_X66WKF3BDVCNJA47I47GXLIN5U/)
- At Yaoundé's Mokolo market (2010 and 2015 fires), a firefighter said connections "are made in total anarchy… wires and cables side by side in legendary disorder", and traders noted no electrical maintenance service. — [Camer.be / Mutations, 12 May 2015](https://www.camer.be/42064/11:1/cameroun-incendies-a-yaounde-branchements-electriques-a-probleme-cameroon.html)
- Illegal electricity connections are "generally jury-rigged, even patched up". Douala (about 6M inhabitants by the author's estimate) has only 2–3 fire companies (opinion column, 2019). — [Camer.be, 10 Dec 2019](https://www.camer.be/78065/32:29/cameroon-pourquoi-y-a-t-il-autant-dincendies-au-cameroun.html)
- Etoa-Meki, Yaoundé, 19 Feb 2026: a mother and five children died in a house fire. A short circuit was among three hypotheses. The article says domestic fires in Yaoundé and Douala kill many people every year, and experts urge electrical inspections. — [AllAfrica / Cameroon press, 20 Feb 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202602200594.html)
- An electrical fire in Garoua destroyed a 16-battery bank in June 2026, showing a new risk from home solar and battery installs (per search-result summary). — [AllAfrica, 29 Jun 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606290396.html)

**Building collapses and construction quality**
- 22–23 July 2023: a building collapse killed 33 people, with 21 survivors rescued. Authorities blamed non-compliance with construction standards, no building permit, and disregard of the land-use plan. (The article's headline says Douala, its body says Ndogbong; the location is inconsistent.) — [Journal du Cameroun, 24 Jul 2023](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/cameroun-douala-de-16-a-33-morts-le-bilan-salourdit-apres-effondrement-dun-immeuble/)
- 10–11 July 2026: an unfinished two-storey building collapsed in **Bonamoussadi (Douala V)**, killing 6 (3 children) and injuring 4. It had been condemned and sealed but was reoccupied. Douala's general causes are listed as non-compliant construction, unplanned growth, **poor-quality materials** and rain. A building under construction also collapsed in Bépanda on 3 June 2026. The city runs a "zero tolerance" demolition campaign. — [Camer.be, Jul 2026](https://www.camer.be/amp/93934/11:1/cameroun-douala-6-morts-dans-leffondrement-dun-immeuble-a-bonamoussadi-cameroon.html)
- A civil engineer lists causes of Douala collapses (Beedi 2020, Ngangue 2019, Bonabéri-Sodiko 2019): inappropriate materials, **wrong cement dosage**, faulty soil studies, ignoring permit plans, and **no site supervision or quality control**. Douala is inventorying buildings without permits. — [Camer.be, 5 Jun 2020](https://www.camer.be/80681/11:1/cameroun-effondrement-des-immeubles-un-phenomene-recurrent-cameroon.html)

**Abandoned jobs, unreliability, fraud and price opacity**
- In the public social housing programme, **535 units awarded to local contractors (SMEs) were abandoned** at Mbanga-Bakoko, Douala, and the housing minister threatened sanctions (search-result summary). — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/78721/11:1/cameroun-logements-sociaux-535-chantiers-abandonnes-a-douala-cameroon.html)
- Buea tailors admit they "do not meet deadlines", which pushes customers toward ready-made imports. Trainees say masters deliberately slow down training to keep cheap labour. — [Frontiers in Education 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1027632/pdf)
- Pricing is described as "inconsistent" and accountability "limited". — [Tony Elumelu Foundation / Maibeta](https://www.tonyelumelufoundation.org/?p=29130). Maibeta also cites "unprofessional conduct" as hurting skilled technicians. — [MIT Solve: Maibeta](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/3333)
- QwickServices describes the service market as affected by **fraud**, and its answer is escrow (pay only after the job is confirmed). — [Cameroon Tribune, Apr 2026](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76197/en.html/details_2)
- Diaspora property projects carry a well-known risk of being cheated without on-site checks. One case involved a relative cutting contact and allegedly taking control of the property. — [Camer.be 2017](https://www.camer.be/61065/15:1/cameroon-cameroun-lorsque-les-biens-dun-mbenguiste-sement-la-zizanie-dans-la-famille.html)
- Classified platforms explicitly warn against sending money (transfer or mobile money) before verification, which implies advance-payment fraud is common. — [CoinAfrique Cameroun](https://cm.coinafrique.com/)
- Contract enforcement is weak and property rights insecure: these are listed constraints for Cameroonian entrepreneurs. — [CEPI 2024/25](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/). Only 2.1% of informal workers have written contracts. — [Ecofin](http://www.ecofinagency.com/public-management/0611-45017-cameroons-informal-workers-earn-an-average-of-cfa83-409-per-month-ins)

### Inferences
- Electrical work is where a bad hire is most dangerous (fire, electrocution) and where demand is most frequent and urgent: power cuts and surges damage installations, and solar and battery systems are spreading. It is also hard for customers to judge the quality of wiring once it is hidden in walls, which is the classic "credence good" case where third-party reputation adds the most value.
- Construction (masonry, site supervision) carries the highest stakes but involves long, high-ticket, multi-actor jobs. That makes it a poor fit for a first pilot, though it is a strong later market (diaspora, landlords).
- The common thread in the fraud cases is advance payment plus no recourse. Milestone or escrow payment via mobile money, plus a public record of completed jobs, targets the documented failure directly.

### Gaps
- No official statistics were found on fires by cause (the "85% electrical" figure is unverified), on electrocutions, or on consumer complaints about artisans.
- No survey data was found on how common advance-payment scams or abandoned jobs are in household repairs. Evidence is anecdotal or comes from startup pitches.
- No source was found that explicitly attributes a specific fire or collapse to a named unqualified artisan or *tâcheron*. Press reports cite standards and materials rather than worker qualifications.
- Price-gouging evidence (for example, a "diaspora price" or "white-person price" premium) was not found in citable form.

## 4. Which institutions regulate, represent or vouch for artisans (artisan law, MINPMEESA artisan card, CCIMA, chambers, associations, tontines, traditional authorities)?

### Takeaway
The formal framework exists on paper but is shallow in practice. Law No. 2007/004 governs crafts, and its 2010 implementing decree sets up registration in Communal Crafts Offices (BCA), which issue a free "carte professionnelle d'artisan". Artisans sit inside the general chamber of commerce (CCIMA). A dedicated Chambre Nationale des Métiers de l'Artisanat has been planned since at least 2026, but its creation decree has not been published. Registration gives few immediate benefits, mainly access to fairs and training, so uptake is low. In practice vouching comes from informal structures: trainers/patrons, njangi/tontine groups, quarter networks and traditional rulers.

### Cited Findings
- **Law No. 2007/004 of 13 July 2007** governs crafts in Cameroon (promulgated by President Biya). Some texts date it 3 July 2007. — [Africa-laws.org copy of Law 2007/004](https://www.africa-laws.org/Cameroon\Comercial%20law\Loi%20N%C2%B0%202007004%20DU%2013%20JUILLET%202007%20REGISSANT%20L%E2%80%99ARTISANAT%20AU%20CAMEROUN.pdf). Its implementing decree is No. 2010/2996/PM of 3 Nov 2010 (per search-result summary; it also defines "virtual crafts galleries", that is, digital promotion spaces).
- **Artisan card / BCA registration** is compulsory and free at the Bureau Communal de l'Artisanat. It requires a location plan of the workshop, a copy of the national ID card and two photos. The card is needed for fairs and exhibitions, training and MINPMEESA support. Crafts are grouped into *artisanat d'art* (art crafts), *artisanat de production* (food processing, leather, textiles, tailoring, cosmetics) and *artisanat de service* (carpentry, mechanics, plumbing, catering, hairdressing and others). In 2025 there were 58 departmental and 10 regional crafts fairs. — [Cameroon Desks, Apr 2025](https://www.cameroondesks.com/2025/04/liste-des-metiers-de-l-artisanat-cameroun-sty.html)
- MINPMEESA is the ministry responsible for crafts. — [Osidimbea: MINPMEESA](https://www.osidimbea.cm/institutions/gouvernement/pme-et-artisanat)
- **CCIMA** (Chambre de Commerce, d'Industrie, des Mines et de l'Artisanat) was created in 1921 and is a consultative body representing commerce, industry, crafts and mining. Christophe Eken was renamed president for 2024–2028 by decree of 19 May 2025 (per search-result summary). — [Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/cameroun-christophe-eken-rempile-a-la-tete-de-la-chambre-de-commerce/); [CCIMA News May–June 2026](https://ccima.cm/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CCIMA-NEWS-MAI-JUIN-2026.pdf)
- **Planned Chambre Nationale des Métiers de l'Artisanat (CNMA)**: MINPMEESA has set up steering committees and drafted decrees, "without success to date". A 2026 op-ed argues registration is costly and burdensome "without any immediate perceived benefit". It notes that artisans without legal existence cannot get bank credit, that skills come from on-the-job learning without certification (which blocks public procurement and subcontracting), and that policy is "too repressive or too fiscal". It proposes a digital professional card linked to a Registre des Métiers (trades register), VAE (validation of prior learning) and CQM certificates, quality labels by sector, links to voluntary CNPS social insurance and microcredit, and a low single "artisan tax". — [Camer.be op-ed, 30 May 2026](https://www.camer.be/93491/30:27/cameroun-creer-une-chambre-de-metiers-comme-levier-de-transition-vers-une-economie-formelle-cameroon.html); syndicated at [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202605300165.html)
- OHADA and MINPMEESA committed to operationalising the OHADA *statut de l'entreprenant* (a simplified status for micro-entrepreneurs) — headline only. — [OHADA.com](https://www.ohada.com/actualite/8465/lohada-et-le-minpmeesa-sengagent-en-faveur-de-loperationnalisation-du-statut-de-lentreprenant.html)
- The minister wants to copy Côte d'Ivoire's crafts model (training, product standards, certification). COMPARISON: Côte d'Ivoire was guest of honour at the 9th SIARC crafts fair in Yaoundé, 27 Jul–5 Aug 2026. — [EcoMatin, Aug 2026](https://ecomatin.net/artisanat-le-cameroun-veut-sinspirer-du-modele-ivoirien-pour-booster-sa-contribution-au-pib)
- **Tontines/njangis** fund 12.9% of informal business start-ups. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- **Traditional authority**: the Fon of Mankon (Bamenda) sits on QwickServices' board and spoke at its launch. — [Cameroon Tribune, Apr 2026](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76197/en.html/details_2)
- **Trade-specific bodies**: CRAMA (Centre de Recherche et d'Appui aux Métiers de l'Automobile) has worked with MINEFOP since 2021 on standards for 2-wheel and light-vehicle trades. The partnership was renewed on 30 Sep 2026 to deliver CQP/DQP certification and **VAE for informally trained mechanics**, under a "one trade, one standard, one diploma" approach. — [fundsforNGOs News, 7 Oct 2026](https://news.fundsforngos.org/2026/10/07/cameroon-minefop-crama-renew-automotive-training-certification-2026/)

### Inferences
- There is no working official credential for most household trades. The artisan card verifies identity and workshop location, not competence, and few artisans hold it. This gap is the opening for a reputation layer, which could also partner with BCA, MINPMEESA or a future CNMA rather than compete with them.
- Customers already trust informal vouching more than state cards: trainer lineage, njangi membership (groups that sanction members who default) and traditional rulers. A platform could formalise this as "vouches" carrying real social accountability. For example, an artisan's njangi group or former patron could co-sign their profile.
- Mechanics are the only household-adjacent trade with an active national VAE and certification channel (CRAMA–MINEFOP) found in this research. That makes mechanics the easiest trade in which to anchor competence verification on an official credential.

### Gaps
- The full text of Law 2007/004 (definitions, master-artisan status, obligations) was not read; only its existence and title were confirmed.
- No data was found on the number of artisan cards issued, BCA coverage or active artisan associations by trade (electricians', plumbers' or tailors' unions in Douala and Yaoundé).
- No source was found on how njangis, *chefs de quartier* or market leaders vouch for individual artisans in practice. This needs fieldwork.
- BEAC/COBAC or consumer-protection rules on service guarantees were not researched (see §8).

## 5. Vocational training and apprenticeship (informal apprenticeship, MINEFOP, CQP/DQP, VAE/RPL, SAR/SM, CFPs)

### Takeaway
Most artisans learn through fee-paying informal apprenticeship with a master (a "madam" or "patron"). It typically runs for years, ends in a graduation or "freedom" ceremony and a certificate, and is not state-recognised. MINEFOP is building a competency-based certification ladder (CQP and DQP certificates, a national qualifications framework, VAE). Delivery is still small next to millions of informal workers: for example, a 2026 PADESCE work-study call offered 646 places.

### Cited Findings
- Buea tailoring study (42 trainers, 86–88% women; 16 apprentices, mean age 22, 69% women; fieldwork Mar–Apr 2022): apprentices pay monthly or annual fees and part of the fee funds a welcome party. Many drop out and return when they can pay, so "some spend several years at the same learning stage". Graduation "validates your skills as a new entrepreneur", and **"certificates and pictures in most shops"** signal quality. The study found limited access to specialised machines, weak literacy among some apprentices, and threats from taxes, power cuts and imports. — [Frontiers in Education 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1027632/pdf)
- Apprenticeship is the main way skills are acquired: trades learned "sur le tas" (on the job) without certification. — [Camer.be op-ed 2026](https://www.camer.be/93491/30:27/cameroun-creer-une-chambre-de-metiers-comme-levier-de-transition-vers-une-economie-formelle-cameroon.html)
- Historical: the ILO studied the informal sector's role in training apprentices in Yaoundé in 1982. — [ILO Research Repository: Demol & Nihan 1982](https://researchrepository.ilo.org/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-modern-informal-sector-in-Yaoundé/995274755802676)
- **CQP/DQP**: MINEFOP holds CQP and DQP defences for learners (e.g. Ndé department, Aug 2026). — [Camer.be 2026](https://www.camer.be/94509/11:1/cameroun-minefop-dans-le-nde-les-jeunes-apprenants-demontrent-leur-savoir-faire-et-leur-creativite.html). PADESCE/MINEFOP "Travail-Études" work-study call: 646 free places, 4–5 months alternating between enterprise and training centre, leading to a CQP (deadline 11 Sep 2026; third-party listing). — [Infospratiques.cm](https://infospratiques.cm/?p=705). The national certification and qualifications framework (CNCQ) is documented by PADESCE. — [PADESCE CNCQ document](https://padesce.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/DOCUMENT-CNCQ-BON-1.pdf)
- **FormPro 237** is an apprenticeship project run by MINEFOP, the French Embassy and AUF, using a competency-based approach and tripartite governance (training centres, companies, the state). Its steering committee met on 29 Jan 2026 (per search-result summary). — [AUF press release](https://www.auf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Communique-de-presse-COPIL-FP237-VF.pdf)
- **VAE for mechanics** is available via CRAMA–MINEFOP (renewed Sep 2026) and linked to the JEME programme ("Jeune, Métier, Emploi"). — [fundsforNGOs News](https://news.fundsforngos.org/2026/10/07/cameroon-minefop-crama-renew-automotive-training-certification-2026/); [JEME portal](https://www.emploijeune.cm/en)
- The World Bank finances a US$125M Secondary Education and Skills Development Project and a US$160M Adaptive Safety Net and Economic Inclusion project (more than 60,000 youths to receive entrepreneurship training). — [World Bank Cameroon overview](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/cameroon/overview)
- Maibeta's model pairs training through educational partners with matching. — [MIT Solve: Maibeta](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/3333)
- Youth literacy (15–24) was 83.3% in 2022, 2.7 points lower than in 2014. — [INS ECAM5 SDG sheet](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Page-ODD-ECAM5-En.pdf)

### Inferences
- Master–apprentice lineage is a strong, native reputation graph that already exists offline. Capturing "trained under X, graduated in year Y, vouched for by X" could bootstrap trust before any platform reviews exist.
- CQP/DQP and VAE give an optional "formal badge" tier that a platform could display or help artisans obtain, for example by referring top-rated artisans to VAE sessions.
- Some apprentices and artisans have limited literacy, so voice, photo and video-based profiles and onboarding are needed.

### Gaps
- No national data was found on the number of informal apprentices, typical apprenticeship length by trade, or fee levels (the EESI3 apprenticeship chapter was not retrievable).
- The SAR/SM (rural artisan and home economics sections) and public CFP (vocational training centre) enrolment statistics (MINEFOP yearbooks) were not reviewed.
- No numbers were found on how many electricians or plumbers hold CQP/DQP certificates, or on any electrician licensing requirement.

## 6. Connectivity: phones, smartphones, internet, data prices, social media and messaging, operators, shutdowns

### Takeaway
Nearly everyone can be reached by mobile phone (32.1M connections, 104% of population, 92% of them broadband-capable, Oct 2026). But fewer than half of people use the internet (14.3M users, 46.3%), only about 1 in 5 has a social-media identity (6.1M, 59% male), and personal phone ownership is about 59% of adults, with big urban/rural and gender gaps. Data costs about US$1.63/GB. Orange (50%) and MTN (47%) split the market, and state-owned Camtel (Blue) has under 3%. The anglophone regions suffered the world's longest internet shutdowns of 2017. Design implication: WhatsApp/voice/USSD-first, low-data, assisted onboarding.

### Cited Findings
- Oct 2026: 32.1M cellular connections (104% of population, +7.7% year on year); 92.2% broadband (3G/4G/5G); **14.3M internet users (46.3%)**; 16.6M people offline (53.7%); 6.10M social-media identities (19.8%; 37.7% of adults); Facebook ad reach 6.10M (fell 450k between Jul and Oct 2026); Instagram 548k; LinkedIn 1.80M; median fixed download speed 15.29 Mbps. — [DataReportal Digital 2027: Cameroon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-cameroon)
- Late 2025: 29.0M connections (96.4%); 87.5% broadband; 12.6M internet users (41.9%); 5.90M social-media identities; adult social-media users **41.0% female / 59.0% male**. — [DataReportal Digital 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-cameroon). Earlier editions: 12.4M internet users (Jan 2025), see [Digital 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-cameroon); 12.73M (43.9%, Jan 2024), see [Digital 2024](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-cameroon). NOTE: the series is revised between editions. Digital 2027's stated growth of +353k does not match the jump from 12.6M to 14.3M, so treat levels as approximate.
- Phone ownership, adults 15+ (2022): **59.4%** (men about 66.8%, women about 53.1%; urban 70.0%, rural 45.2%). Electricity access 60.2% (urban 87.6%, rural 28.3%). — [INS ECAM5 SDG sheet](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Page-ODD-ECAM5-En.pdf)
- Smartphone penetration rose from 25% (2016) to 40% (2020) according to the ART observatory, as cited by the telecoms minister. Orange's "Sanza" smartphone cost CFA 13,900 including taxes (2019). — [Business in Cameroon, 9 Jul 2021](https://www.businessincameroon.com/economy/0907-11756-cameroon-rising-smartphone-adoption-calls-for-actions-to-meet-users-quality-telecom-service-expectations-minpostel-says)
- GSMA data cited by SAMENA: about 15.18M smartphones in use in Cameroon in 2022, against 24.5M mobile subscribers (per search-result summary). — [SAMENA Council daily news](https://samenacouncil.org/samena_daily_news?news=106164)
- Region-wide (GSMA 2024): the mobile internet "usage gap" is 9 times the coverage gap, and smartphone affordability is the single biggest barrier in Sub-Saharan Africa. — [GSMA State of Mobile Internet Connectivity 2024, key findings](https://gsma.com/r/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/The-State-of-Mobile-Internet-Connectivity-Report-Key-Findings-2024.pdf)

Total active users were 31.5M and mobile revenue CFA 630.98bn (+11.89%). Data sold: Orange 348.29M GB, MTN 339.14M GB, Camtel 92.42M GB. Mobile-money revenue: MTN CFA 70.26bn, Orange CFA 65.57bn. Viettel/Nexttel figures were not reported. — [EcoMatin, citing ART 2024 observatory](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-orange-capte-50-du-marche-mobile-et-creuse-lecart-avec-mtn-selon-lart); [Cameroon Tribune: >CFA 1,000bn sector revenue in 2024](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/74465/fr.html/communications-electroniques-plus-de-1000-milliards-de-f-generes-en-2024). MTN claims about 54% share in 2025, an operator claim that conflicts with ART 2024 (per search-result summary). — [EcoMatin](https://ecomatin.net/services-mobiles-mobile-money-parc-dabonnes-mtn-consolide-son-leadership-sur-le-marche-camerounais)
- Camtel had only 67,507 fixed subscribers in 2024 (−12%). — [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-avec-seulement-67-507-abonnes-fixes-camtel-voit-son-parc-chuter-de-12-tandis-que-le-trafic-international-bondit-de-36-547-en-2024/)

- **Data price: US$1.63/GB** (average across all plans, Cable.co.uk survey as reported Oct 2026). - **Shutdowns**: the NW and SW regions lost the internet from 17 Jan 2017 for 93–94 days. - Social media was central to civic mobilisation before the 2025 presidential election (headline). — [Global Voices, Jul 2025](https://globalvoices.org/2025/07/25/social-media-ignites-widespread-civic-engagement-on-the-eve-of-cameroons-presidential-election/)

### Inferences
- About 54% of people are offline and about 41% of adults have no phone of their own. A smartphone-only app would exclude many artisans and a large share of customers outside upper-middle-class districts. WhatsApp (where internet exists), voice calls, SMS and USSD fallbacks are needed. Maibeta reports about 25% of transactions through WhatsApp, SMS and calls.
- The social-media gender skew (59% male) and phone-ownership gaps mean female artisans (tailors, hairdressers) may need assisted onboarding.
- Shutdown history makes the NW and SW (Buea, Bamenda) operationally risky for an internet-dependent pilot. Offline-tolerant features (SMS receipts, cached profiles) are prudent everywhere.
- With Orange and MTN about equal, the platform must support both wallets and both networks from day one.

### Gaps
- No Cameroon-specific WhatsApp penetration figure was found (DataReportal's Cameroon pages did not show WhatsApp, TikTok or YouTube data).
- No current smartphone-versus-feature-phone split was found (latest official figure: 40% smartphone penetration in 2020). The GSMA consumer-survey country data was not accessible.
- No A4AI 1GB affordability figure (% of GNI per capita) for Cameroon was found for 2025–26.
- No public NetBlocks record was found for 2024–26 throttling in Cameroon (e.g. around the Oct 2025 election). This was not verified because the search budget ran out.

## 7. Literacy, languages and the anglophone crisis

### Takeaway
Adult literacy is about 78% (2020) and youth literacy 83% (2022), with large rural and female deficits. Cameroon has about 250–280 languages. French dominates in 8 of 10 regions, including Douala and Yaoundé, where Camfranglais is the urban youth code and Duala or Ewondo are local lingua francas. English and Cameroonian Pidgin dominate the NW and SW (Buea, Bamenda). The anglophone conflict (since 2016–17) has caused 6,000+ civilian deaths and about 700k internally displaced people, many of whom moved to Douala and Yaoundé. Repeated lockdowns and shutdowns have disrupted the regional economy.

### Cited Findings
- Adult literacy (15+) is about 78.2% (2020; men 83.4%, women 73.1%), based on UNESCO UIS data compiled by the World Bank. Other sources give 72.6% for 2018. — [World Bank Gender Data Portal: Cameroon](https://genderdata.worldbank.org/data/download/pdfs/countries/cameroon.pdf); [Macrotrends](https://macrotrends.net/global-metrics/countries/CMR/cameroon/literacy-rate)
- Youth literacy (15–24) in 2022: 83.3% (men 87.7%, women 79.6%; urban 92.1%, rural 70.8%). — [INS ECAM5 SDG sheet](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Page-ODD-ECAM5-En.pdf)
- About 250–260+ languages. The Presidency estimates about 70% of the population speaks French and 30% English. 8 of 10 regions are francophone; the NW and SW are anglophone. 2005 census literacy (12+): French 57.6%, English 25.2%, both 11.6%, neither 28.8%. Lingua francas: Fulfulde in the north (about 12M speakers per Ethnologue), Ewondo in the Centre, South and East (about 578k), Pidgin in the NW and SW. Camfranglais is an urban mixed code. — [Wikipedia: Languages of Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Cameroon)
- In Douala, Duala is the local lingua franca. In 2014, 63.7% of residents aged 15+ could read and write French and 76.4% could speak and understand it. — [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala)
- Cameroon Pidgin English is "probably the most important" regional lingua franca and is used even with low competence in trade. About one fifth to one quarter of people claim it as a first language, up to 40% in the anglophone regions and under 2% in some francophone places. Spelling is not standardised, with competing French-based and English-based conventions. Camfranglais is the identity code of urban francophone youth. — [APICS survey 18: Cameroon Pidgin English](https://apics-online.info/surveys/18); [APICS contribution 18](https://apics-online.info/contributions/18)
- Anglophone crisis: dated from Sep 2017 (separatist declaration 1 Oct 2017), still ongoing. 6,000+ civilians killed (as of Jan 2023), about 700k internally displaced and about 63.8k refugees in Nigeria (Mar 2021). Repeated separatist lockdowns and "ghost town" days (e.g. Buea, 30 Sep 2017). Separatists fought in Buea's Mile 16 and Muea areas in 2018, and Bamenda saw bombings and military operations (2019–20). — [Wikipedia: Anglophone Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_Crisis); [Wikipedia: Buea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buea)
- The IMF counted over 1 million internally displaced people from all causes (violence and climate) as of March 2023. — [IMF 2024](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1cmrea2024002.pdf.md)
- Security problems in the anglophone regions are listed as a business constraint. — [CEPI 2024/25](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/)

### Inferences
- A Douala or Yaoundé pilot needs French as the primary interface language, with tolerance for Camfranglais in voice and chat. A Buea or Bamenda pilot needs English plus Pidgin, and Pidgin's unstandardised spelling favours voice notes over text.
- Displaced anglophone artisans in Douala and Yaoundé lost their home networks, so they especially lack referral reputation. A portable reputation record could help them, and Pidgin/English support in Douala could be a differentiator.
- Literacy gaps (about 22% of adults; worse for rural women) make icon, voice and photo-based UX necessary for the artisan side.

### Gaps
- No reliable speaker counts were found for Camfranglais, Duala or Bamileke languages in Douala specifically.
- No 2024–26 humanitarian figures (OCHA/UNHCR) for NW/SW displacement or for internally displaced people living in Douala and Yaoundé were retrieved.

## 8. Mobile money, cash norms and payment regulation

### Takeaway
Mobile money is mainstream and almost evenly split between Orange Money and MTN MoMo. In 2023 there were 24.2M registered accounts (51% active), 2.4bn transactions and CFA 24.3 trillion in value. In 2025 Orange Money handled 1.43bn transactions (CFA 17.2tn) and MTN 1.29bn (CFA 15.5tn). About 43% of adults use mobile money (2022). Cash remains common for small informal payments, and recent mobile-money taxes (0.2% since 2022, plus CFA 4 per transfer or withdrawal since Jan 2025) push some users back to cash. Wave entered in June 2025 and Camtel is entering the market, which should push fees down.

### Cited Findings
- 2023 (CNEF report): CFA 24,331bn in transactions (about US$42bn, up from CFA 9,271bn in 2019); 2.4bn transactions; **24.23M registered accounts; 51.4% activity rate**; 190,509 points of service. Orange has about 52% of transaction value and volume; MTN holds 55% of balances. Cameroon accounts for 62.83% of CEMAC payment-institution transactions by number. The financial inclusion index rose from 0.19 (2019) to 0.25 (2023). — [EcoMatin, 23 Apr 2025](https://ecomatin.net/mobile-money-le-cameroun-depasse-la-barre-des-24-000-milliards-fcfa-de-transactions-en-un-an-un-record)
- 2025: Orange Money 1.431bn transactions worth CFA 17,176bn (COBAC data); MTN MoMo 1.291bn worth CFA 15,491bn. ### Inferences
- Escrow or milestone payment via MoMo or Orange Money is technically feasible and directly addresses the advance-payment fraud problem. Transaction taxes (0.2% + CFA 4) and withdrawal fees matter for small repair tickets, and artisans often need cash for materials immediately. A hybrid model fits: a small MoMo deposit or escrow for materials, with the balance in cash or MoMo after sign-off.
- Payment records double as reputation and credit-history signals (number of completed paid jobs), which is valuable because informal artisans are excluded from bank credit.

### Gaps
- BEAC/COBAC rules were not verified in this session. From background knowledge (unverified here): CEMAC Regulation No. 04/18/CEMAC/UMAC/COBAC (2018) governs payment services and e-money issuers, and holding customer funds in escrow may require a licence or a partnership with a licensed issuer or bank. This needs legal confirmation.

## 9. Demand side: who hires artisans (middle class, diaspora remittances and home building, landlords, SMEs, property managers)?

### Takeaway
Demand is concentrated among urban salaried households (Yaoundé has 64.6% salaried employment, Douala 48.5%), landlords and property developers in fast-growing districts, SMEs and offices, and the diaspora building homes remotely. Remittances were about US$690M in 2024 (1.3% of GDP), down from about US$787M in 2023. Middle-class size was not found in citable form. Douala's upscale and middle-class districts include Bonapriso, Kotto and Bonamoussadi (north Douala, a growing housing area with new malls).

### Cited Findings
- Personal remittances received were about US$690M in 2024, down from about US$787M in 2023 and up from about US$242M in 2015. — [Statistics of the World, citing World Bank WDI](https://statisticsoftheworld.com/country/cameroon/remittances-received) (USD values per search-result summary). Crédit Foncier du Cameroun offers home loans to the diaspora (2015, per search-result summary).
- Salaried employment is 48.5% in Douala and 64.6% in Yaoundé (2021). — [INS EESI3 Phase 1](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/)

The enterprise stock was 350,422 in 2022 and over 444,000 in 2024; 16,845 new SMEs were added in 2025 (headline). — [Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/entrepreneuriat-pres-de-350-000-pme-estimees-au-cameroun-en-2022/); [CEPI](https://camepi.org/report-state-of-entrepreneurship-in-cameroon-2024-2025/); [EcoMatin](https://ecomatin.net/le-tissu-des-pme-camerounaises-sest-etoffe-de-16-845-nouvelles-entreprises-en-2025)

### Inferences
- The best early customers are salaried households and landlords in middle-class residential districts. They have recurring maintenance needs (electrical, plumbing), MoMo wallets and smartphones, and the most to lose from a bad hire. Diaspora clients are a high-value second segment for remote project supervision (photo/video proof, milestone escrow), but their projects (construction) are bigger and riskier.
- Landlords and property managers with multiple units are natural "anchor" customers who can generate repeat volume and reviews quickly.

## 10. Recommendation: the ONE trade and ONE town/neighbourhood for a launch pilot

### Takeaway
**Recommended pilot: domestic electricians (installation, repair and surge-damage fixes, with solar/inverter wiring as an add-on) in Douala's northern residential belt: Bonamoussadi, with adjacent Makepe and Kotto (Douala V arrondissement).** Electrical work combines the highest documented safety cost of a bad hire, frequent and urgent demand, quality that customers cannot judge themselves (the textbook case for third-party reputation), and an emerging certification route (CQP/VAE). Douala-North offers the densest mix of middle-class landlords and households, the country's highest urban unemployment (spare artisan capacity), French plus Camfranglais (and Pidgin among anglophone migrants), stable connectivity, and none of the shutdown or conflict risk of Buea or Bamenda. **Runner-up: tailors in Buea-Molyko**, the best-documented artisan cluster with a native lineage-based trust system, but a smaller market with security and shutdown risk.

### Cited Findings (evidence used for the choice)
**Why electricians**
- Fires linked to chaotic wiring, low-end electrical equipment and power cuts recur in Yaoundé, Douala and Bafoussam, including fatal house fires in 2026. — [Le360 2025](https://afrique.le360.ma/societe/cameroun-linquietante-recrudescence-des-incendies-dans-les-grandes-villes-du-pays_X66WKF3BDVCNJA47I47GXLIN5U/); [Camer.be 2015](https://www.camer.be/42064/11:1/cameroun-incendies-a-yaounde-branchements-electriques-a-probleme-cameroon.html); [AllAfrica 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202602200594.html)
- Experts explicitly recommend "a certified electrician" and audits, but no registry or credential is visible to consumers. — [Camer.be 2026](https://www.camer.be/amp/93595/11:1/cameroun-rallonge-defectueuse-le-danger-silencieux-qui-tue-dans-nos-maisons-cameroon.html)
- Every Cameroonian matching startup lists electrical work and plumbing first (Maibeta, QwickServices, Nofey, FixIt, TrustWork). — [TEF](https://www.tonyelumelufoundation.org/?p=29130); [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76197/en.html/details_2); [WeAreTech](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/audrey-ludivine-baha-launches-nofey-to-digitize-access-to-local-artisans-in-africa)
- 87.6% of urban residents have electricity, so nearly every urban home is a potential customer. — [INS ECAM5](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Page-ODD-ECAM5-En.pdf). Repairs saw one of the largest increases in informal-sector investment between 2010 and 2021 (+CFA 251,400). — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/61372/fr.html/details_2)
- Home battery and solar installs carry new fire risk (Garoua battery-bank fire, Jun 2026). — [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606290396.html)
- A formal certification route exists for building a "verified" tier (CQP/DQP, national qualifications framework, VAE model shown for auto trades). — [PADESCE CNCQ](https://padesce.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/DOCUMENT-CNCQ-BON-1.pdf); [fundsforNGOs](https://news.fundsforngos.org/2026/10/07/cameroon-minefop-crama-renew-automotive-training-certification-2026/)

**Why Douala, and Bonamoussadi/Makepe/Kotto specifically**
- Douala is the economic capital and commercial hub of the CEMAC region, with about 3.8M people in the city proper (2025) and about 4.2M in the agglomeration (2026). — [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala); [UN WUP via Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/population/largest-cities-in-cameroon/)
- It has the highest unemployment in the country (15.4%) and the highest labour participation (59.2%), so supply is plentiful and underused. — [INS EESI3 report](https://ins-cameroun.cm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/EESI3_Rapport-principal_Emploi_VF.pdf); [INS page](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/)
- Bonamoussadi is the main northern housing area with new malls and supermarkets, and Kotto is the middle and upper-class hub, giving a concentrated, solvent, maintenance-heavy demand pool. — [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala)
- The Bonamoussadi collapse (July 2026) and earlier Douala V collapses (Beedi 2020) make building safety and workmanship a salient local issue now. — [Camer.be 2026](https://www.camer.be/amp/93934/11:1/cameroun-douala-6-morts-dans-leffondrement-dun-immeuble-a-bonamoussadi-cameroon.html); [Camer.be 2020](https://www.camer.be/80681/11:1/cameroun-effondrement-des-immeubles-un-phenomene-recurrent-cameroon.html)
- Douala is a French-dominant city (63.7% of adults read and write French) with a Duala lingua franca and a mixed anglophone migrant population. — [Wikipedia: Douala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douala)
- Classifieds listings are concentrated in Douala and Yaoundé. — [CoinAfrique](https://cm.coinafrique.com/)
- A competitor, QwickServices, launched in Douala in April 2026, confirming demand, but with a broad 2,000-category model rather than deep trade-specific verification. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76197/en.html/details_2)

**Why not the alternatives (evidence)**
- *Buea-Molyko / Bamenda*: 2017 shutdowns of 93+ days and about 4 months more; an ongoing conflict with lockdowns and fighting in Buea's Mile 16 and Muea areas; Bamenda's growth has slowed to 0.70% a year; Buea is small (about 232k). — [Access Now](https://www.accessnow.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Cameroon_RE-Internet-Connectivity.pdf); [Wikipedia: Anglophone Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_Crisis); [UN WUP via Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/population/largest-cities-in-cameroon/). Buea's strengths are the Molyko tailoring cluster serving university staff and students, lineage-based trust, Maibeta's 2015 origin and the Silicon Mountain tech talent. — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1027632/pdf); [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/3333); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2018/1/26/cameroon-internet-shutdowns-cost-anglophones-millions)
- *Yaoundé (Bastos/Biyem-Assi)*: the largest agglomeration (5.24M) with the highest salaried share (64.6%), so a strong customer base. But it already hosts Quicky and OmniJobber, it has lower unemployment (11.7%) than Douala, and the documented electrical-fire evidence is similar. It is a strong second city to expand into. — [INS](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/); [WeAreTech](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/cameroons-quicky-services-bridges-clients-and-providers-via-mobile-app); [MIT Solve OmniJobber](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/2777)
- *Mechanics*: the CRAMA–MINEFOP VAE channel is a plus. — [fundsforNGOs](https://news.fundsforngos.org/2026/10/07/cameroon-minefop-crama-renew-automotive-training-certification-2026/). But customers come to the garage, so the home-visit trust problem (letting a stranger into the house, hidden wiring quality) is weaker, and garages are already visible and locatable.
- *Tailors*: low safety stakes, a female-majority supply base, and a documented reliability problem (missed deadlines). — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.1027632/pdf). A good wedge for reputation (repeat business, photo portfolios) but lower willingness to pay for verification.

### Inferences
- Electricians maximise the gap between how much a good reputation is worth and how hard quality is to observe: the customer cannot check wiring, failures are dangerous, and need is frequent and urgent (outages, surges). That makes verified reputation most valuable there.
- Plumbing has the same home-visit and hidden-work traits, and many Douala artisans advertise as "électricien-plombier". A natural phase-2 extension in the same neighbourhoods is plumbing.
- Pilot mechanics suggested by the evidence:
  - Recruit artisans door to door, the way OmniJobber did.
  - Seed vouching from trainer lineage, njangi groups and quarter leaders.
  - Offer optional CQP/VAE badges.
  - Run on WhatsApp, voice and USSD rather than a smartphone-only app.
  - Use milestone MoMo/Orange Money escrow for the materials deposit.
  - Interface in French, with Camfranglais and Pidgin tolerated in voice.
  - Anchor demand through landlords and property managers in Bonamoussadi, Makepe and Kotto.
  - Track fire-safety outcomes, such as earthing and breakers installed, as a public-good metric.
- Risk: QwickServices is already in Douala. Differentiation must come from depth of verification and authentic local vouching in one trade and one area, not from breadth of categories.

### Gaps
- No neighbourhood-level demand data was found (household counts, incomes or repair spending for Bonamoussadi, Makepe or Kotto). The choice rests on qualitative descriptions and city-level statistics.
- No count of electricians in Douala and no electrician association contacts were found. Fieldwork is needed to size supply in the pilot area.
- Legal position of a platform vouching for electrical safety (liability) and of escrow under COBAC rules is unverified.
- Recommendation confidence: moderate. The trade choice is well supported. The neighbourhood choice is a reasoned judgement that a short field scan (artisan density, landlord interviews) should validate against Yaoundé (Biyem-Assi, Bastos) before launch.
