## 1. Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL/VAE) and skills assessment for informal workers

### Takeaway
Across Africa, governments are building RPL routes that let informal artisans prove skills through practical tests, portfolios and screening instead of school certificates. Uptake is still small next to the size of the informal workforce. Cameroon has the legal basis for VAE (2019 law, 2020 decree) and is piloting sector RPL with the ILO, but no artisan VAE pathway is operating at scale yet. A platform should therefore build its own credible, practical, community-anchored verification and link it to state certification (CQP/VAE) where that exists, rather than wait for state certification.

### Cited Findings
**ILO and global guidance**
- ILO notes that women and men in the informal economy often have no formal proof of their skills. It says RPL assessment can ease the move into formal work and further learning — [ILO, Skills for transitions to formality](https://www.ilo.org/skills-transition-formality)
- The ILO RPL Learning Package (Geneva, 2018) sets out the "building blocks" of an RPL system for governments, social partners, assessors and facilitators — [ILO RPL learning package](https://www.ilo.org/publications/recognition-prior-learning-rpl-learning-package)
- The ILO's resource guide *Assessing skills in the informal economy* (2015, ISBN 978-92-2-128944-9) draws on nine case studies from Africa, Asia and Latin America. It concludes that "bottom-up skills assessments are feasible and yield multiple benefits" (productivity, consumer protection, social cohesion) but "need to be managed well to be effective" — [ILO 2015 resource guide](https://www.ilo.org/publications/assessing-skills-informal-economy-resource-guide-small-industry-and)
- ILO guidance on costing and financing RPL breaks costs into direct, support and opportunity costs and sets out financing models. This matters because cost is a major barrier for artisans — [ILO RPL costing guidelines](https://www.ilo.org/resource/report/guidelines-costing-and-financing-recognition-prior-learning)
- A 2026 ILO workshop report ("From Experience to Opportunity") says RPL lets informal-economy workers, migrants and displaced people have their skills formally recognised — [ILO 2026 RPL workshop report](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/RPL%20workshop%20report%20layout.pdf)
- **[Elsewhere: Ethiopia]** Ethiopia launched a national RPL guideline with ILO support in September 2024 for people who learned non-formally or informally and hold no certificate — [ILO Ethiopia RPL](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/recognition-prior-learning-ethiopia)
- ILO's *Upgrading informal apprenticeship: a resource guide for Africa* (Hofmann, 2012, 178 pp.) is the reference text for strengthening informal master–apprentice systems "from within" — [ILO research repository](https://researchrepository.ilo.org/esploro/outputs/encyclopediaEntry/Upgrading-informal-apprenticeship/995341493102676). A 2026 ILO guidance note, *Designing apprenticeships*, updates the 2012 guide for African practitioners — [ILO Designing apprenticeships 2026](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Designing-apprenticeships-EN_Web.pdf). ILO calls informal apprenticeship "by far the most important source of skills training in Africa and South Asia" — [ILO skills transitions page](https://www.ilo.org/skills-transition-formality)

**[Elsewhere: Kenya] NITA trade tests and RPL**
- NITA has run trade tests since 1959 in more than 40 trade areas and assesses about 60,000 candidates a year, most of them informal-sector youth. Only Grade III currently works like RPL: it is an open test that asks for no evidence of how skills were learned. This has discouraged experienced artisans from sitting higher grades — [NITA: Kenya to implement RPL](https://nita.go.ke/news/NITA/346-kenya-to-implement-recognition-of-prior-learning.html)
- KNQA developed an RPL Policy Framework in 2020. A Presidential Directive (1 June 2021) told awarding bodies such as NITA to roll out RPL — [NITA: Kenya to implement RPL](https://nita.go.ke/news/NITA/346-kenya-to-implement-recognition-of-prior-learning.html); [ILO PROSPECTS: Kenya's inclusive national RPL framework](https://prospectslearnings.ilo.org/node/184)
- NITA's RPL sequence is awareness, then screening (application form, chosen grade, placement questions, supporting documents), then portfolio assessment ("RPL practitioner examines evidence submitted by the candidate against prescribed learning outcomes"), then actual assessment, then certification. It was piloted with KNQA and ILO in Dadaab and Garissa — [NITA: Implementation of RPL](https://www.nita.go.ke/news/NITA/355-implementation-of-recognition-of-prior-learning-rpl.html)
- The first RPL certificates went to 62 candidates in Kwale (July 2022). A later pilot screened 636 candidates in 8 counties, identified through the Kenya National Federation of Jua Kali Associations (KNFJKA), IRC and the Department of Refugee Services. Trade associations therefore acted as the mobilisation channel — [NITA: RPL extended pilot screening in 8 counties](https://nita.go.ke/news/NITA/354-rpl-extended-pilot-roll-out-nita-undertakes-screening-of-rpl-candidates-in-8-counties.html)
- In October 2025 NITA began a national RPL awareness drive aimed at more than 40,000 youth, linked to the Skills Initiative for Africa — [The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/amp/education/article/2001529885/kenya-rolls-out-rpl-certification-for-40000-youth-to-boost-employability); national RPL standards are at [KNQA National Standards](https://knqa.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/National-Standards-1.pdf); and jua kali uptake is reported at [Kenya News Agency, 2026](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/recognition-of-prior-learning-a-blessing-to-jua-kali-artisans/)

### Inferences
- The pattern across Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, South Africa and Benin is consistent: **practical demonstration + portfolio + screening interview + (optional) knowledge check**, with **trade associations as mobilisers and master artisans as assessors**. A platform can copy this structure at low cost: a recorded practical "show me" test judged by two association masters against a short trade checklist.
- National RPL output is tiny compared with demand (Kenya targets tens of thousands, against millions of informal workers). For a 2026–27 Cameroon pilot, state certification can only be a top-tier badge held by a minority, not a gate to joining.
- Benin's CQM replacing the master-issued *diplôme de libération* shows that master-signed graduation documents already carry social legitimacy in francophone West and Central Africa. Cameroon has comparable master–apprentice "libération" customs, but this is **not verified for Cameroon in this research** (see Gaps). A platform could digitise the master's attestation as one vouching signal.
- The ILO's Cameroon HIMO RPL work validated "candidate portfolios". A platform portfolio format aligned with MINEFOP occupational standards could later count as evidence in a VAE file. Agreeing a data format with MINEFOP early is a strategic opportunity.

### Gaps
- No official MINEFOP procedure, fee schedule or eligibility rules were found for artisan VAE or CQP-by-experience. The CNCQ text was not read in full.
- Not verified: whether Cameroonian master artisans issue a "libération" certificate or hold a ceremony, or how GICAM, the Chambre de Commerce, d'Industrie, des Mines et de l'Artisanat (CCIMA) and trade associations (mechanics, tailors, hairdressers) organise. Search budget ran out.
- Nigeria RPL certification counts appeared in search snippets (e.g., "about 500 nationally"), but the original source could not be confirmed, so they are excluded.

## 2. Proof of work: portfolios, geotag/timestamp, job confirmation, OTP, QR, practical tests

### Takeaway
Geotagged, timestamped photos are useful but easy to forge if read from file metadata. Evidence counts only if it is captured live in the app, server-timestamped, hash-checked and tied to a customer-confirmed job (OTP or confirmation from the customer's phone). Hardware-signed provenance (C2PA) is arriving on premium phones but will not reach most Cameroonian artisans' low-end Android phones soon. So the design should rest on **customer-side confirmation plus server-side checks**, not on device trust.

### Cited Findings
- EXIF DateTimeOriginal is written by the device clock and can be edited with free tools. Mock-location apps are legally sold developer tools, and spoofing leaves no obvious traces in a standard photo file — [TrueScreen: certified geolocation](https://truescreen.io/articles/certified-geolocation-court-admissible-photo-evidence/)
- Vendor mitigations: block gallery uploads (live camera only), detect duplicates through hash matching, and timestamp each submission server-side — [Timemark photo verification](https://www.timemark.com/features/photo-verification). Note: vendor marketing.
- Forensic practice requires integrity hashes, verifiable timestamps and signed acquisition logs (ISO/IEC 27037 chain of custody) — [TrueScreen](https://truescreen.io/articles/certified-geolocation-court-admissible-photo-evidence/)
- Google Pixel 10's camera reached C2PA Assurance Level 2. Signing keys sit in StrongBox/Titan M2, with on-device trusted timestamps. Android Key Attestation lets Google verify "a genuine app on a secure device" without knowing who uses it. Google says Content Credentials are "not the sole solution" — [Google Security Blog, Sept 2025](https://security.googleblog.com/2025/09/pixel-android-trusted-images-c2pa-content-credentials.html)
- Third-party SDKs and apps (Truepic Lens, Nodle Click) add C2PA credentials on ordinary Android and iOS phones — [Content Authenticity Initiative: Click](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/community-story-click)
- **[Elsewhere: India]** Urban Company requires a selfie when the professional arrives at the customer's home, checked against their identity with Microsoft Azure face verification, so that "jobs are not passed onto someone else". This is part of a 10-point safety system that includes OTP checks — [Urban Company blog](https://www.urbancompany.com/blog/urban-company-strengthens-safety-protocol-using-microsoft-azure-cognitive-services); [AuthBridge on Urban Company](https://authbridge.com/?p=2119)
- Urban Company onboarding includes background verification and training of 3–45 days depending on prior skill. Candidates are tested on technical and soft skills before approval — [Urban Company: upskilling](https://www.urbancompany.com/blog/setting-up-service-partners-for-success-upskilling-at-urban-company). Services carry a guarantee, e.g., "30 day service guarantee" on appliance repair — [Urban Company refrigerator repair page](https://urbancompany.com/delhi-ncr-refrigerator-repair-kalkaji-new-delhi)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Lynk vetted "Pros" on technical and soft skills before letting them quote, and built its own Lynk Academy because partnerships with vocational training providers "never took off" — [The Flip: Lynk lessons](https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Lynk's warning on past reputation: one Pro with 500 five-star jobs would "go mteja" (disappear) on the 501st. Vetting did not prevent failed jobs — [The Flip](https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/). Mercy Corps adds that "too many jobs still went wrong for well-recommended or vetted individuals", and that customers would not pay for vetting and convenience alone — [Mercy Corps/Prosper Youth Impact Labs case studies](https://www.mercycorps.org/sites/default/files/2020-12/Youth-Impact-Labs-Partner-Case-studies.pdf) (via search snippet; the full PDF was too large to fetch)
- Practical tests: Kenya's Grade III open trade test needs no evidence of how skills were learned — [NITA](https://nita.go.ke/news/NITA/346-kenya-to-implement-recognition-of-prior-learning.html). Nigeria's NSQ uses a performance test to set the level — [Fed Poly Nekede](https://fpno.edu.ng/sdc-directorate). Benin's CQM tests practice and theory — [Gouv.bj](https://www.gouv.bj/article/2425/lancement-examen-renove-session-octobre-2023-nouvelle-ere/)

### Inferences
- **Job-confirmation protocol (design):** (1) the customer books, or the artisan logs a job with the customer's phone number. (2) On arrival or completion, the customer reads a one-time code from SMS or USSD to the artisan, or taps "confirm" in the app. This ties the job to a distinct real phone number. (3) The artisan takes before and after photos in the app (no gallery uploads). Photos are hashed and server-timestamped and coarse location is recorded. (4) The customer gives a binary rating plus tags, by voice or tap. Only jobs that pass steps 1–3 count as **"verified jobs."**
- **QR at the workshop:** a printed QR or short code on the workshop sign lets walk-in customers confirm jobs and leave feedback. This captures the large offline job flow, not only jobs booked through the platform. The workshop's location becomes a fixed, verifiable anchor, checked once by a field agent or association leader.
- **Anti-gaming:** cap how many confirmations one phone number can give the same artisan, and flag clusters (the same few numbers confirming many jobs, or confirmers who are all contacts of each other). This is a light version of the Sybil detection BrightID uses (Section 3).
- C2PA could be an optional bonus signal for artisans with capable phones. It must not be required, or it would exclude low-end phone users and widen inequality.
- Lynk's experience shows a platform-backed **service guarantee** may build customer trust more than individual vetting. Verification should feed a guarantee, not replace one.

### Gaps
- No independent academic study was found measuring how well geotag/photo verification resists gaming in informal-labour settings. The evidence is mostly from vendors.
- Smartphone versus feature-phone shares among Cameroonian artisans were not found here (likely covered by another researcher).

## 3. Peer vouching and social collateral

### Takeaway
Theory and microfinance evidence show that social ties can stand in for collateral (social collateral). But formal joint liability is not necessary: public, repeated interaction often does the work. Web3 proof-of-personhood systems show how to make vouching costly and Sybil-resistant: vouchers stake something and lose it if the person they vouched for proves fake or misbehaves, connections must be diverse, and attestations expire. For Cameroon, the natural pre-trusted vouchers are trade-association heads and masters, market leaders, quarter chiefs and religious leaders. Vouching should be **staked, diverse, expiring and auditable**.

### Cited Findings
- Besley & Coate (1995): joint liability creates "social collateral" that substitutes for missing physical collateral, enforced through peer pressure and social sanctions — summarised in [Dhami et al., CESifo WP 7773](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp7773_0.pdf); review in [Ghatak & Guinnane, "Economics of Lending with Joint Liability"](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fth/yalegr/791.html)
- Ghatak (2000): joint liability works as a screening device and "will induce endogenous peer selection" (assortative matching of safe borrowers) — [Ghatak, Economic Journal 2000](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/ghatak/ej.pdf)
- Karlan (FINCA Peru), using quasi-random group formation, found evidence that peers successfully monitor and enforce joint-liability loans — [Karlan, Group Banking (J-PAL)](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/publications/95_Karlan_Group_Banking.pdf)
- Karlan, Mobius, Rosenblat & Szeidl (QJE 2009), "Trust and Social Collateral": network connections act as collateral, and trust between two people equals the most one can borrow from the other via network paths. Dense networks create bonding capital for valuable exchanges, and loose networks create bridging capital for information. In job-recommendation networks, strong ties between employers and recommenders reduce information gaps about candidate quality — [EconPapers QJE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:124:y:2009:i:3:p:1307-1361.)
- **Counter-evidence:** Giné & Karlan randomly converted half of 169 group-liability centres in the Philippines to individual liability. Repayment did not change and the centres grew more, but weekly public meetings were kept — [Giné & Karlan, CGD WP 111](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cgd/wpaper/111.html). Grameen II likewise dropped joint liability but kept repayment in public meetings — [World Bank paper](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/4e072c29-575d-5250-a89a-5fe88127dac4/download)
- **Proof of Humanity** (design document): registered users vouch that an applicant exists and is not a duplicate, and should only vouch for people they "physically encountered". Social-graph analysis is proposed as a challenge or filter tool — [PoH design doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1z01MS0-h75ESVmWymU2Gv3Z43p35oZAFtQLStOeu7Ek/export?format=pdf). (Not covered here: PoH also requires a deposit that challengers can win. That point comes from background knowledge and was not verified in this run.)
- **BrightID:** verification comes from social connections that must be diverse ("not all from same cluster"), with Sybil detection by graph algorithms such as SybilRank that look for star-shaped fake clusters. Coordinated fake networks can still game it — [BlockEden course](https://blockeden.xyz/course/defi/11.4/); [Chainscore comparison](https://www.chainscorelabs.com/comparisons/identity-soulbound-vs-verifiable-credentials/proof-of-personhood-systems/proof-of-humanity-vs-brightid-social-verification-implementations) (secondary sources with inconsistent figures)
- **Gitcoin/Human Passport:** each "Stamp" is a verifiable credential with a fixed weight. The score is the sum of weights. Stamps expire after 90 days. The recommended "unique human" threshold is 20 (maximum 100), and weights are re-tuned over time — [Human Passport stamp weights](https://support.passport.human.tech/stamps/stamp-weights); [Gitcoin: how the score is calculated](https://support.gitcoin.co/gitcoin-knowledge-base/gitcoin-passport/common-questions/how-is-gitcoin-passports-score-calculated); [Passport key terms](https://docs.passport.human.tech/overview/key-terms)
- **EigenTrust:** trust is transitive (if i trusts j, i also trusts whom j trusts). Local trust values are normalised so malicious peers cannot inflate each other arbitrarily, and the computation is seeded with pre-trusted peers. Critics note that randomly chosen pre-trusted peers give unrepresentative values for peers far from them in the network — [Kamvar, Schlosser & Garcia-Molina, WWW 2003](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~hlutfiyy/610/papers/eigentrust.pdf); [EigenTrust overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EigenTrust)
- **[Cameroon]** Tontines/njangi run on social trust and exclusion: failures are rare and self-correcting because defaulters face social exclusion — [Fair Observer](https://fairobserver.com/?p=34530). Foretia Foundation argues regulation should adapt to community savings circles rather than impose formal rules — [Foretia Foundation](https://www.foretiafoundation.org/alternative-financing-models-community-savings-circles-and-rotating-funds-for-youth-startups-in-cameroon/)
- **[Cameroon]** Neighbourhood security in poor Yaoundé quarters runs largely through informal local arrangements — [JSSJ, security in two Yaoundé neighbourhoods](https://www.jssj.org:443/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/JSSJ4-9-en.pdf). After the 2017 attacks, Douala neighbourhoods set up self-defence committees, some of which abused residents, which led to purges of members — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/amp/63232/11:1/cameroun-lutte-contre-le-terrorisme-douala-sous-haute-surveillance-cameroon.html)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** KNFJKA (jua kali federation) identified RPL candidates for NITA, so trade federations already work as a de facto vouching layer for state certification — [NITA](https://nita.go.ke/news/NITA/354-rpl-extended-pilot-roll-out-nita-undertakes-screening-of-rpl-candidates-in-8-counties.html)
- Kiva reports a 96.4% average repayment rate through lending partners, but its own page did not describe social underwriting or trustee mechanics — [Kiva: How it works](https://www.kiva.org/about/how)

### Inferences
- **Staked vouching (design):** each voucher (a master artisan, association leader, quarter chief, pastor or imam, market leader, or a Tier-3+ artisan) gets a limited vouching budget, e.g., 5 active vouches. If a vouched artisan gets an upheld complaint for fraud, no-show with a deposit or abuse within 6–12 months, the voucher loses reputation points and possibly vouching rights. Vouches expire after 12 months unless renewed, as Passport stamps expire. This copies the social-collateral mechanism without financial joint liability, which Giné & Karlan suggest may be unnecessary.
- **Diversity rule:** require vouches from at least two *different* trust circles, e.g., (a) trade (master or association) plus (b) residence (quarter chief or neighbour) or faith community. This reduces collusion, following BrightID's "not all from same cluster" principle.
- **Seeded trust propagation:** run a personalised EigenTrust or PageRank-style score seeded with **pre-trusted local anchors** (verified association heads, partner NGOs, field agents) per city or quarter, not one global seed. This follows the critique that seeds far from a peer give poor estimates.
- **Risk of elite capture:** quarter chiefs and self-defence committees can be abusive or partisan (Douala evidence). Chief vouching should be one signal among several, never a gate, and artisans should be able to appeal.
- The Karlan et al. finding that strong ties help job recommendations supports "people from your quarter hired him" signals (Section 5).

### Gaps
- No evaluation was found of staked vouching in a services marketplace specifically. The evidence is from microcredit and Web3 identity.
- The Proof of Humanity deposit and challenge mechanics, BrightID's current status, and Kiva's "social underwriting" repayment data were not verified (search budget ran out).
- The formal roles of Cameroon's chefs de quartier (e.g., issuing residence attestations) were not confirmed.

## 4. Reputation system theory: what works, what breaks

### Takeaway
Raw star averages inflate over time, cluster near the top (Airbnb about 4.7, eBay a median of 100% positive) and hide bad experiences, because unhappy customers stay silent and both sides fear retaliation. Robust designs (1) take reviews only from verified transactions, (2) reveal both sides' reviews at the same time, (3) count silence (eBay "effective percent positive"), (4) shrink small samples toward a prior (Bayesian/Beta, Wilson lower bound), (5) weight recent behaviour more, (6) give new entrants a fair way in, and (7) watch for bias. Five-star scales show racial bias that a binary thumbs scale removed in a 2025 *Nature* study.

### Cited Findings
**Foundational evidence**
- Resnick & Zeckhauser (eBay 1999 data): feedback was almost always positive, buyer and seller feedback were strongly correlated (reciprocation and retaliation), and eBay's net feedback score "encourages Pollyanna assessments" and "is far from the best predictor available" — [Resnick & Zeckhauser](https://presnick.people.si.umich.edu/papers/ebayNBER/index.html). 89% of buyer–seller pairs were one-shot — [Resnick & Zeckhauser PDF (Harvard)](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/rzeckhauser/files/trust_among_strangers.pdf)
- Controlled field experiment: an established high-reputation seller sold more, at higher prices, than a new seller with identical goods, but the effects were modest — [Harvard Magazine](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/print/1624?page=all)
- Tadelis (2016) reviews how feedback systems support trust on eBay, Taobao, Uber and Airbnb, and the problems of bias in them — [Tadelis, Annual Review of Economics 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/anr/reveco/v8y2016p321-340.html)

**Rating inflation and silence**
- Filippas, Horton & Golden: on an online labour market, feedback scores rose sharply over time, causing top-censoring. Much of the rise reflects raters lowering their standards (35–45% in the 2017 version; at least 50% in later slides; published in *Marketing Science* 2022). P2P platforms are more exposed than product-review platforms — [Horton: Reputation Inflation](https://john-joseph-horton.com/papers/reputation-inflation/); [CESifo WP 6750](https://cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp6750.pdf); [INFORMS release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Five-Star-Rating-New-Research-Finds-It-May-Not-Reflect-What-Consumers-Receive)
- Zervas, Proserpio & Byers: the average Airbnb rating is about 4.7, with about 94–95% of properties at 4.5+ stars. Airbnb ratings were higher than, and weakly correlated with, TripAdvisor ratings for the same properties — [Zervas et al. paper](https://people.bu.edu/zg/publications/airbnbreviews.pdf); [BU summary](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2015/at-airbnb-where-every-stay-is-above-average)
- Nosko & Tadelis: the median eBay seller shows 100% positive (10th percentile 98.21%). "Effective percent positive" (EPP = positive feedback ÷ all transactions) has a mean of 64% and a median of 67%, because buyers with bad experiences often leave silently and quit the platform. Putting EPP into search ranking was field-tested on eBay — [NBER w20830](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20830); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/making-bid-for-more-accurate-ebay-seller-profiles)

**Reciprocity and double-blind reviews**
- Fradkin, Grewal & Holtz (*Marketing Science* 2021): Airbnb's simultaneous-reveal experiment hid each side's review until both submitted or the window closed. Review rates rose (reported as +9.7% host and +1.8% guest reviews), ratings fell slightly, reviews became more candid, and there was no detectable effect on later transactions. Airbnb rolled it out to everyone — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2939064); [NBER conference draft](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f81348/f81348.pdf). Caveat: some authors were Airbnb employees.

**Fake reviews and verified-purchase gating**
- Luca & Zervas: about 16% of Boston restaurant reviews on Yelp were filtered as suspicious. Restaurants with few reviews or weak reputations were more likely to commit fraud, and competition increased fake negative reviews — [Luca & Zervas, Management Science (Harvard DASH)](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/22836596)
- TripAdvisor cannot verify that a reviewer actually stayed at a hotel, which is the main way fake reviews get through. Booking-gated platforms only accept reviews from verified guests — [One Mile at a Time](https://onemileatatime.com/insights/fake-tripadvisor-review/). (Mayzlin, Dover & Chevalier, AER 2014, found more promotional reviews on TripAdvisor than on booking-gated Expedia. This is from background knowledge; the paper was not retrieved in this run.)

**Scoring maths**
- Wilson score lower bound: rank by the lower bound of a confidence interval on the true positive fraction, (p̂ + z²/2n − z√[(p̂(1−p̂) + z²/4n)/n]) / (1 + z²/n). This stops items with few ratings outranking well-rated items with many. It applies to binary (positive/negative) ratings, not 5-star scales — [Evan Miller](https://evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html)
- Beta Reputation System (Jøsang & Ismail, 2002): combines positive and negative feedback with a beta probability density. Its expected value, (r+1)/(r+s+2) for r positive and s negative outcomes, gives a reputation that naturally handles small samples, and the paper includes "forgetting" factors that discount old feedback. Its strength is "flexibility and simplicity as well as its foundation on the theory of statistics" — [Jøsang & Ismail, Bled 2002](https://mn.uio.no/ifi/english/people/aca/josang/publications/ji2002-bled.pdf). The Dirichlet generalisation handles multi-level ratings — [Jøsang & Haller, ARES 2007](https://mn.uio.no/ifi/english/people/aca/josang/publications/jh2007-ares.pdf). See also the survey — [Jøsang, Ismail & Boyd, DSS 2007](https://idtrust.xml.org/resource/a-survey-of-trust-and-reputation-systems-for-online-service-provision)
- Recency windows in practice: Uber averages a driver's most recent 500 rated trips — [Uber deactivation policy](https://www.uber.com/za/en/blog/driver-deactivation-policy/)

**Locally calibrated thresholds**
- Uber sets a minimum average rating **per city** "because there are cultural differences in the way people in different cities rate each other" — [Uber](https://www.uber.com/za/en/blog/driver-deactivation-policy/). Minnesota's 4.5 is reportedly the lowest in the US — [Minnesota DLI](https://dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/tnc_uber_driver_access_to_platform_092623.pdf)

**Cold start**
- Pallais (AER 2014): hiring inexperienced oDesk workers, and giving them more detailed public evaluations, substantially improved their later employment. Firms under-hire newcomers because the information a first job creates benefits other employers too — [NBER w18917](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18917)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** On Lynk, public ratings meant customers chose Pros with the most jobs and highest ratings, which hurt new Pros. Moving trust to the platform brand plus a guarantee helped new Pros get work — [The Flip](https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/)

**Fairness and bias**
- Hannák et al. (CSCW 2017): on TaskRabbit and Fiverr, workers perceived as Black got worse ratings than similar workers perceived as White. Perceived gender and race correlated with search rank on TaskRabbit (correlational) — [Hannák et al.](https://khoury.northeastern.edu/home/ancsaaa/files/CSCW17.pdf)
- Botelho, Jun, Humes & DeCelles (*Nature* 2025): on a North American home-services platform (100,759 jobs, 69,971 ratings), non-white workers earned about 91¢ per white worker's dollar under 5-star ratings (4.72 vs 4.79). **Switching to binary thumbs up/down eliminated the earnings gap.** In experiments, positive ratings were near-identical (95.9% vs 95.4%) — [Rice University news](https://news.rice.edu/news/2025/racial-earning-gap-gig-work-eliminated-through-new-rating-system)
- Yale SOM: rating systems can amplify existing user bias — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/ratings-systems-amplify-racial-bias-on-gig-economy-platforms). A GroupLens simulation found no bias, but its authors say the setup may have suppressed it — [GroupLens](https://grouplens.org/blog/simulation-experiments-on-the-absence-of-ratings-bias-in-reputation-systems/)
- Fairwork Kenya 2023: ratings "are often biased towards customers, leaving platform workers to face the consequences of low ratings" — [Fairwork Kenya 2023](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2023/12/Fairwork_kenya_Report_2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Binary plus tags beats 5 stars** for this pilot. It resists inflation-driven top-censoring, the *Nature* evidence shows it reduces discrimination, and it is easier for low-literacy users (thumbs or smiley icons). Add 2–3 tap tags ("on time", "price as agreed", "clean work", "would hire again") to recover detail. Generalising the *Nature* result from race in North America to ethnicity, gender or anglophone/francophone identity in Cameroon is an assumption to test.
- **Score = Beta posterior with recency decay** over *verified* jobs only. Display a conservative lower bound (Wilson or Beta 10th percentile) as "% satisfied", next to the **count of verified jobs**, rather than a 4.9-style average.
- **Count silence:** like EPP, confirmed jobs with no feedback should weigh slightly toward neutral or negative, or at least be shown ("42 confirmed jobs, 30 rated, 29 👍").
- **Simultaneous reveal** of artisan→customer and customer→artisan ratings, with a fixed window (e.g., 7 days), to cut retaliation, given the two-sided risks in Section 7.
- **Local calibration:** set badge thresholds per city or trade from local distributions, as Uber does per city, because rating norms differ (e.g., Douala vs Yaoundé, or tailoring vs plumbing).
- **Cold-start policy:** a "New, verified by [association]" label, guaranteed exposure in rotation for new verified artisans, and a platform guarantee on first jobs. Pallais shows the first-job information is a public good the platform should subsidise.

### Gaps
- No African-market empirical study of rating inflation or double-blind reviews was found. All the core evidence is from US and EU platforms (eBay, Airbnb, Upwork, TaskRabbit).
- No study measured gender bias in artisan ratings in Africa specifically.
- The Airbnb "J-curve" (ratings piling up at 5 stars, few in the middle) is reflected in the Zervas et al. distribution, but no separate Airbnb engineering-blog source was retrieved.

## 5. Customer trust signals for low-literacy users

### Takeaway
For low-literacy users, research supports text-free, icon-and-voice interfaces with consistent help. Voice forums can scale but bring harassment risks for women. The strongest trust cues to show are visual and social: photo of the artisan and their work, a count of verified jobs, badges with simple pictograms, voice testimonials, a platform guarantee, and endorsements from the customer's own quarter or network, which follows trust-transfer and network-trust theory.

### Cited Findings
- Medhi, Sagar & Toyama: text-free UIs (graphics plus voice feedback, built through ethnography with illiterate domestic workers in Bangalore slums) were strongly preferred to text UIs. One prototype was a **job-search app for domestic labourers**. They flag a consistent help feature as an overlooked need — [Medhi et al., ITID 2007](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse490d/09au/papers/Text-FreeUI.pdf)
- Textual literacy correlates with Raven's test scores, and both predict how well people navigate UI hierarchies even in text-free UIs. This argues for flat, shallow navigation — [Medhi Thies 2015 survey](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse490c/18au/readings/medhi-thies-2015.pdf)
- Sangeet Swara (CHI 2015): community-curated voice forum where callers vote on posts for playback — [Vashistha et al. CHI 2015](https://billthies.net/assets/pdf/chi15-sangeet-swara.pdf). 53,000 calls from 13,000 users, about 80% of whom had never used social media — [CACM 2019 "Internet of the Orals"](https://aghaaliraza.com/publications/2019--CACM--The_Internet_of_the_Orals.pdf)
- Women on voice forums faced abusive content, flirting, threats and harassment, which suppressed their participation — [Vashistha et al. CHI 2019](https://ictd.cs.washington.edu/docs/papers/2019/vashistha_chi2019_threat.pdf)
- CGNet Swara: voice-based citizen journalism suits communities where low literacy limits other media — [Mudliar, Donner & Thies, ITID 2013](https://www.billthies.net/assets/pdf/mudliar13itid.pdf)
- Trust transfer (Stewart 2003): trust in an unknown party transfers from a trusted, linked party according to how strongly they are perceived to interact and how similar they are. A business with a physical presence can inherit trust from its offline channel — [Stewart, Organization Science 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v14y2003i1p5-17.html). Grouping and entitativity affects transfer — [Stewart & Malaga, ICIS 2003](https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2003/90)
- Network-based trust predicts informal borrowing, and strong ties improve the quality of job recommendations — [Karlan et al. QJE 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:124:y:2009:i:3:p:1307-1361.)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Lynk found that trust signals beyond individual ratings mattered: upfront pricing, prepayment, a two-hour arrival target, a standard service process, branded uniforms and PPE, and links with reputable suppliers — [The Flip](https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/)
- **[Elsewhere: Nigeria/Ghana/Kenya]** Jiji's "Verified" badge means ID-plus-selfie KYC (SmileID) but is shown only to sellers with paid boost packages. Independent guides stress that the badge does not guarantee honesty — [Jiji FAQ](https://jiji.ng/faq/125); [Marketplace.ng guide](https://www.marketplace.ng/blog/how-to-verify-online-sellers-in-nigeria)

### Inferences
- **Artisan card for low-literacy users (design):** large face photo; a trade pictogram; 3–6 recent in-app job photos; the **verified-jobs count shown as a number plus dots**; a thumbs-up share with an icon; tier badges as distinct coloured shapes with a short voice explanation on tap; a 15–30 s **voice testimonial** from a confirmed customer (moderated, with a women-only or no-reply option to avoid harassment); and a **"neighbour" line** ("3 people from Bonamoussadi hired him") computed from verified jobs. The neighbour line puts trust transfer and local network trust into practice.
- **French/English/Pidgin voice prompts**, with an interactive voice response (IVR) or USSD fallback for feature phones, follow from the Swara evidence. This assumes Cameroonian artisans and customers have similar literacy constraints, which needs local confirmation.
- Never tie the verification badge to paid promotion (Jiji's model). It erodes what the badge means.

### Gaps
- No Cameroon-specific usability study of trust signals was found. Nielsen Norman Group trust-signal guidance was not retrieved in this run.
- No direct evidence was found on how effective "neighbour endorsement" displays are (Nextdoor-style) (search budget ran out).

## 6. Customer trust in digital platforms in Africa and Cameroon

### Takeaway
Across Africa, people use platforms faster than they come to trust them. Pay-on-delivery, offline touchpoints and recourse mechanisms were key to early e-commerce trust. In Cameroon, scams, cybercrime, non-delivery and mobile-money phishing are the main trust constraints. Repeated good experiences, and operators visibly monitoring risk, build continued use more than institutional assurances.

### Cited Findings
- **[Elsewhere: Nigeria]** Jumia and Konga introduced pay-on-delivery to get people to try online shopping. A former Jumia Nigeria chair described cash on delivery as one of several trust measures, alongside offline engagement and recourse mechanisms — [Stanford eCorner transcript](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/marketplaces-operate-on-trust-transcript.pdf). Analysts note customers choose PoD "because of trust issues" — [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/951834/jumia-murder-nigerian-e-commerce-businesses-rethinking-payment-on-delivery-for-customers)
- **[Elsewhere: Nigeria]** A survey of 277 Jumia customers concluded providers must build trust and reduce perceived risk — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bco/mbraaa/v8y2021p65-75.html)
- **[Cameroon]** A Yaoundé I thesis found transaction security, the merchant's reputation and competence, and internet familiarity best explain online purchase confidence. Fraud, non-delivery and card theft were key concerns — [DICAMES](https://dicames.online/jspui/handle/20.500.12177/7353)
- **[Cameroon]** A Buea survey (n=250) found cybercrime has eroded trust in Cameroonian e-commerce and willingness to transact — [EconPapers 2025](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bjf:journl:v:10:y:2025:i:5:p:537-549). Other small studies report rising adoption with mixed experiences — [IJEMR](https://ijemr.vandanapublications.com/index.php/ijemr/article/view/410); [MRU start-up study](https://cris.mruni.eu/cris/handle/007/45572)
- **[Cameroon]** A PLS-SEM survey of 273 households found that **trust built from repeated good experiences and operators actively monitoring risk** drove continued mobile-money use. Institutional trust and some regulatory actions had limited effect — [Abertay University](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/the-role-of-cybersecurity-and-trust-on-customers-continuous-usage/)
- **[Cameroon]** Sophisticated phishing successfully targets mobile-money accounts, and countermeasures should address emotion and social interaction — [Njoya et al., EUDL](https://eudl.eu/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-34896-9_26). The "sent by mistake, please refund" scam is common, and MTN warns never to enter a PIN for a transaction you did not start — [The Voice](https://www.thevoicenews.net/?p=3849)
- **[Cameroon]** A survey of 807 mobile-money agents in the Centre region cited fraud and scams as pressures. Revenues fell after the 0.2% mobile-money tax in 2022 — [ICTD](https://www.ictd.ac/blog/cameroon-mobile-money-agents-struggling-to-increase-revenue/)
- **[Cameroon]** Mobile-money subscribers grew from about 2.1 million (2013) to about 8 million (2017) — [O'Reilly/ISTE chapter on scamming risks in Cameroon](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/perceptions-and-analysis/9781786307446/c05.xhtml). Commentary warns that mobile money can be used for coerced payments, citing a 2025 Yaoundé case — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/92950/11:1/mobile-money-au-cameroun-quand-le-paiement-digital-devient-une-arme-de-sequestration-cameroon.html)
- **[Elsewhere: Africa]** Caribou Digital's Platform Livelihoods research (700+ interviews, 16 platform case studies) shows young Africans combine several platforms with off-platform hustles. Platforms are "structures they must navigate" — [Caribou Platform Livelihoods Project](https://caribou.global/projects/platform-livelihoods-project/); [Caribou "The hustle is real"](https://medium.com/caribou-digital/platform-livelihoods-of-young-people-in-africa-the-hustle-is-real-c566b3b3a7f9)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Financial-service partners expected better data quality than platforms of informal workers could provide. Lynk acted on this — [CGAP](https://cgap.org/node/5951); [IFC Lynk case](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/mgrt-pub/202008-d2e-lynk.pdf); [GSMA Lynk case](https://www.gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Lynk-Connecting-informal-workers-to-job-opportunities-in-Kenya.pdf)
- **[Elsewhere: Nigeria/Kenya]** Jiji safety advice: never pay in advance, meet in public, check feedback, and be wary of requests to move off-platform — [Jiji safety tips](https://jiji.ng/safety-tips.html); [Jiji account security](https://jiji.ng/faq/account-security)

### Inferences
- In Cameroon the platform should **show that it is visibly watching for risk** (fraud warnings, a quick "report" button, visible action on reports), because the Abertay result found this drives continued use more than regulatory reassurance.
- **Payment design:** offer mobile-money escrow (deposit held, released on customer OTP confirmation) as an option, keep cash-on-completion available, and never ask for PIN-like actions in-app. This copies pay-on-delivery logic while protecting artisans (Section 7).
- Payment-integrated job confirmation (a mobile-money reference tied to a job) is a strong proof-of-work signal and harder to fake than a rating.

### Gaps
- No nationally representative survey of Cameroonian trust in digital marketplaces or MTN/Orange Money was found. ART or GSMA data were not retrieved.
- Afrobarometer interpersonal-trust data for Cameroon (trust in neighbours, relatives, strangers) was not retrieved (search budget ran out).

## 7. Trust in the other direction: protecting artisans from non-paying or abusive customers

### Takeaway
African platform-work audits (Fairwork) repeatedly find that ratings favour customers, deactivations happen without due process, and appeal channels respond poorly. A credible artisan platform must therefore rate customers too, give artisans due process and appeals, protect against non-payment (deposits or escrow), and prevent discriminatory or retaliatory ratings from destroying a livelihood.

### Cited Findings
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Fairwork's fair-management principle requires a documented process where workers can be heard, can appeal decisions (including deactivation) and are told the reasons. Kenya 2021: workers faced arbitrary deactivation without due process — [Fairwork Kenya 2021](https://www.bmz-digital.global/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Fairwork-Kenya-2021-Report.pdf). Kenya 2022: only 3 of 9 platforms (Glovo, Little, SweepSouth) showed due process — [Fairwork Kenya 2022](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2023/02/230202_fairwork_kenya-report-2022_RZ_small.pdf). Kenya 2023: ratings are "often biased towards customers" — [Fairwork Kenya 2023](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2023/12/Fairwork_kenya_Report_2023.pdf)
- **[Elsewhere: Nigeria]** Communication channels on all platforms were "not very responsive", and workers reported deactivation without warning (2022). Arbitrary deactivations were observed again in 2023 — [Fairwork Nigeria 2022](https://www.bmz-digital.global/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Nigeria-Rating-2022.pdf); [Fairwork Nigeria 2023](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2023/07/Fairwork-Nigeria-Ratings-2023-report-red-1.pdf)
- Two-sided rating precedent: Uber began deactivating **riders** with low ratings in 2019 — [WEF](https://weforum.org/stories/2019/05/uber-will-start-deactivating-riders-with-low-ratings)
- Airbnb's simultaneous reveal reduced retaliation in two-sided reviews — [Fradkin et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2939064)
- **[Elsewhere: Kenya]** Lynk used **prepayment** and upfront pricing, which also protected Pros from non-payment and haggling. Some customers distrusted tradespeople because of past bad experiences or class differences — [The Flip](https://theflip.africa/newsletter/lynk-lessons-learned-the-hard-way/)
- Women users of voice platforms faced harassment and threats — [Vashistha et al. CHI 2019](https://ictd.cs.washington.edu/docs/papers/2019/vashistha_chi2019_threat.pdf)
- **[Cameroon]** Mobile money can be used for coercion, and operators reportedly lack duress detection (commentary) — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/92950/11:1/mobile-money-au-cameroun-quand-le-paiement-digital-devient-une-arme-de-sequestration-cameroon.html)

### Inferences
- **Customer reputation:** artisans rate customers with binary plus tags ("paid as agreed", "respectful", "safe site"). Repeat non-payers or abusers are flagged to other artisans, with deposits required from flagged customers.
- **Deposit or escrow for materials**, released on OTP completion. Disputes go to a **local mediation panel** (association representative plus platform staff, with a community leader if both parties agree). This draws on the local dispute norms used in tontines and associations.
- **Due-process charter, Fairwork-aligned:** no automatic deactivation from ratings alone. Written reasons, a 14-day appeal and human review are required. Ratings from customers flagged for abuse, and statistical outliers (e.g., one customer giving many negatives), are down-weighted.
- **Safety features for women artisans** (hairdressers, tailors, cleaners) and women customers: share-trip/visit with a trusted contact, a panic or report button, the option to accept only verified customers, and moderated voice testimonials.

### Gaps
- Specific worker-protection features of SweepSouth, Lynk and similar home-services platforms (client vetting, SOS buttons, insurance) were not retrieved (search budget ran out).
- No Cameroon data was found on non-payment rates or customer abuse of artisans.

## 8. Recommended verification ladder and reputation-scoring approach for a Cameroon pilot (researcher's synthesis)

### Takeaway
Recommendation (synthesis of Sections 1–7, not a tested finding): a **five-tier ladder** that moves from identity, to community vouching, to proof of work, to a practical skill test by masters, to sustained verified performance, with state certification (CQP/VAE) as a parallel top badge. Pair it with a **binary-plus-tags, Beta-posterior, recency-decayed score computed only from OTP-confirmed jobs**, shown as "verified jobs + % satisfied (conservative) + neighbour endorsements". Add **staked vouching**, **two-way ratings with simultaneous reveal**, **local calibration**, **cold-start exposure** and **due process**.

### Cited Findings
(Each design element is grounded in a source cited above; key anchors repeated here.)
- Practical test, portfolio and screening is the common RPL structure — [NITA](https://www.nita.go.ke/news/NITA/355-implementation-of-recognition-of-prior-learning-rpl.html); [QCTO](https://www.qcto.org.za/assets/certification-infographic-final-2021-approved.pdf); Cameroon validated "candidate portfolios" in its 2026 HIMO RPL work — [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/cameroon-lays-foundation-recognizing-skills-gained-through-labour-based)

### Inferences

**A. Verification ladder (badges): proposed for the Douala/Yaoundé pilot**

| Tier | Badge (pictogram) | Requirements | What it signals | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Registered** (grey circle) | Phone number + OTP; name matches mobile-money account name; live selfie; trade; workshop/quarter declared | A real, reachable person | Urban Company selfie check; Jiji KYC |
| 1 | **Known locally** (green house) | ≥2 vouches from *different* circles: (a) trade (master/association head/market leader) **and** (b) residence or faith (chef de quartier, pastor/imam, existing Tier-3 customer-neighbour). Workshop location checked once by an agent or association | "People here know him/her" | Social collateral; BrightID diversity; KNFJKA mobilisation |
| 2 | **Work shown** (camera) | ≥5 in-app, server-timestamped, hash-unique job photos **and** ≥3 jobs confirmed by OTP from distinct customer numbers (at most 1 per household) | Has really done this work recently | Proof-of-work protocol (Section 2) |
| 3 | **Skill tested** (hammer + check) | Recorded practical "show me" test (30–90 min, a standard task per trade) scored by **2 association masters** with a 10–15-item checklist based on MINEFOP occupational standards. **Or** holds a CAP/CQP/DQP/VAE certificate (verified by photo plus issuer check) | Competence judged by peers to a standard | NITA/NSQ/QCTO/CQM practical tests; ILO bottom-up assessment |
| 4 | **Trusted pro** (gold star) | ≥20 verified jobs over ≥6 months; conservative satisfaction ≥ local trade threshold; upheld-complaint rate below threshold; no unresolved disputes | Sustained reliability | Beta/EPP scoring; local calibration |
| Parallel | **State certified** (flag) | MINEFOP CQP/VAE or other official qualification verified | Formal recognition; bridge to VAE | 2019 law and 2020 decree; HIMO RPL |

Ladder rules:
- **Vouch staking:** each voucher has at most 5 active vouches. An upheld fraud, abuse or no-show-with-deposit complaint against the vouchee within 12 months costs the voucher reputation, and after 2 such events, vouching rights. Vouches expire at 12 months (renew with one tap).
- **Badges expire or decay:** Tier 2 needs ≥1 verified job per 90 days to stay active (like Passport's 90-day stamp expiry). Tier 3 tests are re-taken every 3 years or when switching trade.
- **Association role:** associations nominate masters as assessors and receive a small fee per test, but **cannot alone** grant Tier 1 or Tier 3 (two-circle and two-master rules). This limits favouritism.
- **Women and newcomers:** allow a faith or women's-group leader as circle (b) and a female master assessor on request. Track tier progression by gender and region monthly.

**B. Reputation score: proposed computation**

1. **Eligible feedback:** only from OTP- or payment-confirmed jobs. At most one counted rating per customer phone per artisan per 30 days. Repeat hires count as an extra "rehire" signal.
2. **Rating format:** 👍 / 👎 plus optional tags (on time, price kept, quality, clean, respectful), given by tap or 10-second voice. Feedback is revealed **simultaneously** to both sides after both submit or 7 days pass.
3. **Core score (Beta posterior with decay):**
   - For each job i: weight wᵢ = exp(−ln2 · ageᵢ / 365), a 12-month half-life (parameter to tune). Multiply by 0.5 if the rater is flagged or an outlier, and by 1.2 if the rater is a "neighbour-network" customer (shares a quarter or vouch path) **only for display of the neighbour count, not the score**, to avoid in-group bias.
   - R = Σ wᵢ·[👍], S = Σ wᵢ·[👎] + λ·Σ wᵢ·[confirmed, no feedback] with λ ≈ 0.25 (EPP-style silence penalty; tune).
   - Prior: α₀ = 4, β₀ = 1 (a new verified artisan starts at about 80% expected satisfaction; prior strength 5). Optionally set the prior per tier, e.g., Tier 3 α₀ = 6.
   - Expected satisfaction E = (α₀ + R) / (α₀ + β₀ + R + S). **Displayed "% satisfied" = 10th-percentile of Beta(α₀+R, β₀+S)**, or the Wilson lower bound, so thin records are not overstated.
4. **Ranking and matching:** combine the conservative score, distance or quarter proximity, tier, response time and a **personalised trust boost**: a short-path EigenTrust/PageRank from the searching customer's own vouch and hire graph, seeded by local pre-trusted anchors (association heads, partner NGOs). Cap the boost so it cannot override bad performance.
5. **Display (low-literacy):** face; trade icon; **"34 jobs checked ✔"**; "92% 👍" (conservative); top 2 tags as icons; **"5 people from your quarter hired her"**; badges; voice testimonials; "Guarantee" shield for the first job.
6. **Cold start:** new Tier ≥1 artisans get guaranteed rotation slots (e.g., 10–15% of impressions in their trade and area) and a platform-backed first-3-jobs guarantee (re-do or refund up to a cap), following Pallais and Lynk.
7. **Local calibration:** badge and ranking thresholds per city × trade, from percentiles of the local score distribution, recalculated quarterly (Uber's per-city logic). Compare Douala and Yaoundé before adding anglophone cities.
8. **Integrity:** cluster detection on the confirm/vouch graph (dense small cliques, same-device numbers), velocity limits, a hash check for reused photos, and visible "under review" states.
9. **Two-way and fairness:** customers carry their own score (paid as agreed, respectful). No automatic deactivation. A Fairwork-style written reason, 14-day appeal and human review. A monthly bias audit of 👍 rates and ranking exposure by gender, age, region and language group. Change the design if the gaps persist.

**C. Pilot evaluation suggestions**
- A/B test binary versus 5-star ratings and simultaneous versus sequential reveal. Measure feedback rate, the spread of scores, the customer re-hire rate, and gender gaps.
- Track what share of complaints involve vouched versus unvouched artisans, and voucher loss events, to calibrate the staking penalty.
- Check practical-test reliability: inter-rater agreement between the two masters (target kappa ≥ 0.6, an assumption to validate) and correlation of the Tier 3 score with later complaint rates.

### Gaps
- All numeric parameters (half-life, prior α₀/β₀, λ silence penalty, vouch budget, thresholds) are **design starting points, not empirically derived for Cameroon**. They need calibration in the pilot.
- No evidence was retrieved on how Cameroonian customers read badges or icons, or on whether quarter chiefs and associations will act as vouchers or assessors and on what terms (fees, liability).
- The legal treatment of a platform-issued skills badge under the CNCQ, and whether MINEFOP would accept platform portfolios as VAE evidence, is unknown.
- Data protection: Cameroon's personal-data rules on selfies, location and voice data were not researched here.
